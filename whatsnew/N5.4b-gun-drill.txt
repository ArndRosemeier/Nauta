The gun crews now work their guns in front of you: the muzzle men sponge, hand up the cartridge and ram, the tackle men heave the gun out and haul her in, and the gun captain crouches behind to lay her. Their faces strain at the heaving; at the shot the captain cries and the others flinch.
A burning wad now flies out of the muzzle with each shot, and the guns tip up and down as they are laid.
To see it: beat to quarters in ?sail and look at the guns from the waist or the gun standpoint.

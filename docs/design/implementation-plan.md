# Nauta: implementation plan

What is built, in which order, and when each piece is done. The design is in [nauta-plan.md](nauta-plan.md); this file turns its build order (N0 to N7) into milestones and work packages that a coding thread can pick up one at a time.

Written 2026-11-17 by the design thread. Arnd decides at the gates; design questions go to the design thread.

## How work is done

**Roles.** The design thread owns this plan and the design. Coding threads build work packages. Arnd judges each milestone at its gate, on the iPad.

**A coding thread takes one work package** (`N1.3`), named in its first message. Before writing code it reads [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), [nauta-plan.md](nauta-plan.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), [SEAMS.md](../SEAMS.md) and the package below with everything it depends on.

**Each package lands like this:**

1. Work on a branch. Keep `npm test` and `npm run build` green.
2. Add the package's tests, its rows in SEAMS.md, its owners in `tools/guards/rules.json`, its numbers as named constants or rule tables, and its what's-new note `whatsnew/N1.3-<what>.txt` ([whatsnew/README.md](../../whatsnew/README.md)): what Arnd can now see or do on the iPad, in plain words, with no version number.
3. Take the package's screenshots or run its check, and look at them.
4. Open a pull request with `PLAN: N1.3` and a `COPIES:` line, and squash-merge it into `main` once CI is green (one commit is one build, one version). No review by Arnd is needed for a package. `main` deploys to https://arndrosemeier.github.io/Nauta/.
5. In the same pull request, set the package's status in this file to `done` and note anything the next package must know under "Notes" of its milestone.

**Stop and ask the design thread** (through Arnd) when a package needs a decision this plan does not make, when "done when" cannot be met as written, or when the work wants to change a fixed rule, a budget or another package's seam. Do not decide design in a coding thread.

**Status values:** `open`, `in work` (with the thread's name), `done`, `blocked` (with the reason).

**A milestone is finished at its gate:** Arnd opens the deployed page on the iPad Air M4 and judges the gate's list. Until he says yes, the milestone stays open and the next one's packages that depend on it wait.

**The frame's shares fit together at the worst moment** (design thread, 2026-11-17, after the ship design's warning that sea 5, crew 5, ship 3 and smoke 3 ms left nothing for the rest). A broadside in smoke with both crews in sight is sea 4 + crew 4 + ships 3 + smoke 2 + sky, light and interface 2 = 15 ms, which leaves 1.7 ms spare in the 16.7 ms frame. How each share is kept: the sea's compute costs next to nothing on the iPad (the probe), so its 4 ms is shading, and the part of the sea hidden behind the ship and the smoke is not shaded; men below deck are drawn only through openings (the ship's rooms and openings, N2.4), and distant and hidden men animate at a lower rate (N4.0 fixes the rates); smoke is drawn at half resolution and joined to the frame by depth; and one render scale, a quality setting, is the first knob when a frame runs long. No share may borrow from another: a package that cannot keep its share comes to the design thread.

`?bench` (package N1.9) measures the frame and memory budgets on the device itself and shows the result on screen, since the tablet has no developer tools at hand.

## Cross-cutting rules for every package

- **The simulation is the game.** Everything that decides anything lives in `src/sim`, pure and seeded, stepped at a fixed rate. The renderer and the sound only show state and events.
- **One state, serialisable.** The whole match is one plain data value (`SimState`) that can be sent, saved and compared. No simulation state hides in a closure or a class instance elsewhere.
- **Events for what happens in an instant** (a gun fires, a spar parts, a man falls): the simulation emits them, drawing and sound consume them. A package that adds something audible or visible adds its event, never a callback into the renderer.
- **Rules as data** in `src/sim/rules/` (one file per subject). A package that needs a tuning number adds it there, with unit and source.
- **Quality levels** come from one place (`src/app/quality.ts`, N1.8). A package that costs frame time adds its knob there; it never checks for "tablet" itself.
- **Sound with the feature.** Each package lists its sounds; they are made through `@futuremagic/audio`'s tool and OpenRouter into `sounds.json`, several seeded takes each. No voices.
- **Touch first.** Every interaction a package adds works with a finger before it gets a key.
- **Shared code moves, it is not copied.** Code that MasterWorld already has and Nauta needs is moved into a shared package by a pull request in AssetGenerator, then both games use it.

## N1 The sea

The new ocean: better than MasterWorld's from deck height, in calm, breeze and gale, within its 4 ms on the tablet. Everything later floats on it, so it is built with the most care.

**The design in one paragraph.** The sea is one function of seed, wind and time. Its long waves (those a hull feels) are a seeded set of wave components computed in `src/sim` on the CPU, the same on every machine. The renderer displaces the surface by exactly these components, so a hull sits in the water it is drawn in. Shorter waves, ripples, foam and light are added by the renderer on top and never affect the simulation.

Nauta's `SkyView` (render/skyView.ts) extends it with its settings and the clock; view.ts makes the sun and hemisphere lights before the warm-up. For N1.4: the sky's environment map is `sky.envTexture` (the env copy is drawn at `ENV_SATURATION` of its colour; a mirror puts it back with `fullSky`), the sun's light as it falls is `sky.sunColor` and `sky.lightDirection`, the day's light `sky.light` (`SkyLight`); the scene's lights carry `sky.gain`. **Notes:** N1.6 (wind) is in `src/sim/wind.ts`, its tuning in `src/sim/rules/wind.ts` (forces 0 to 8) and the Beaufort scale in `src/sim/rules/beaufort.ts` (one table for wind and sea). A match's wind is `makeWind(seed, force, fromBearing)`, plain data. `meanWind(state, t)` is the wind without gusts (the sea's driver); `windAt(state, x, z, t)` adds the gusts (the sails'). Both give `{ speed, toward, x, z, from, gust }`: `toward` is the sea's angle (0 = +x, a quarter turn = +z), `from` a compass bearing (0 north = -z, clockwise, `units.ts#bearingDir`). The mean veers slowly, each swing taking 10 to 30 minutes, within the preset's limit (45° in calm down to 10° from force 6), and freshens a little; whether the sea follows the veer or keeps its start value is the sea's choice. For N1.4: `gustsNear(state, x, z, t, radius)` lists the patches (heart, radius, strength now, peak), and the shader draws each with `gustShape` (one at the heart, (1 - d²)² to the rim); patches drift with the mean wind. Gust, veer and freshen numbers are first proposals, to be tuned by eye at N1.4 and by feel at N3.3.

N1.1 (sea state) is in `src/sim/sea/`, its tuning in `src/sim/rules/sea.ts`. A sea is `startSea(seed, wind, t)`, plain data; call `followWind(sea, meanWind(windState, t), t)` every step and the sea takes a new wave set only when the mean wind has moved by 1.5 m/s or 15°, fading it in over 3 minutes (`changeWind` forces one). Each wave set is 25 Gerstner waves (`SEA_COMPONENTS`; with the swell's 3 the sea sums 28, and 32 missed the 1 ms on CI) from a JONSWAP spectrum (0.6 to 2.5 times the peak frequency) with a cos-2s spread, equal energy per wave, so the set's significant height is the preset's. For N1.3: `seaAt(sea, x, z, t)` is a calm point and where its water is now, exactly what the vertex stage must do: for each layer, factor `f` from `layerWeights(sea, t)`, and for each wave θ = kx·x + kz·z − omega·t + phase, height += f·amp·cos θ, shift −= f·reach·(kx, kz)·sin θ, plus f²·lift per layer (the lift keeps the mean level at zero under Gerstner's wide troughs). The renderer's short waves start below the set's `shortest` wavelength. For N2.2: `surfaceAt(sea, x, z, t)` is the water under a world point (Newton on the shift, about 1.2 µs each against 0.5 µs for `seaAt`), with normal and water velocity; `stretch` is below 1 on crests and never reaches 0 (the simulated sea does not fold; foam from folding is the short waves'). Sines and cosines in the hot loop are `trig.ts#sincos`, built from + and × only and without a branch, so they round alike on every engine and cost a third of Math.sin plus Math.cos; the wave set itself uses Math.exp and Math.cos once per set, which engines may round differently in the last bit, so machines should compare the host's state, not sea samples. Answered by the design thread below (N1.10 adds a swell; heights are judged at N1.4).

N1.10 (the swell) is in `src/sim/sea/swell.ts`, its tuning (`SWELL_*`) in `src/sim/rules/sea.ts`. Every sea carries `seaSwell`, one more wave set beside its layers: 3 waves of 9 to 12 s, 0.6 m significant height together, drawn once by `startSea` from the sea's seed and the starting wind's direction (every wave at least 40° off it, within 8° of the swell's own direction), never changed by `changeWind` or `followWind`, the same at every force. For N1.3: `seaAt` adds the swell first at factor 1 (no fade), with its own `lift` (f² = 1), then the layers as before; so the vertex stage sums `seaSwell` plus each layer by `layerWeights`. The swell's waves are 125 to 225 m long, so on a small patch of sea it shows as the whole surface rising and falling slowly. The wind set's `shortest` still marks where the renderer's short waves begin. The wind sets' fold limit stays 0.7; the swell takes what two crossfading sets leave below 1 (`SWELL_FOLD_LIMIT`, about 0.01), which holds its choppiness near 0.7 to 0.8. A preset's height becomes √(preset² + 0.6²): a calm 0.6 m, force 3 0.85 m, force 6 3.06 m.

N1.3 (the sea's surface mesh) is in `src/render/`: `seaGrid.ts` lays the sea out as a quadtree of square tiles around the eye (CDLOD's layout), every tile one shared grid of 16 × 16 cells, level k being 4 m × 2^k wide on a fixed lattice of its own size (so vertices never swim), 0.25 m cells under the eye, drawn out to 1.25 times the horizon's distance plus 500 m. A level reaches 5 tile sides from the eye; in its last fifth its vertices slide onto the next coarser grid (`seaMorph`, mirrored in the vertex stage), so levels meet without cracks and tiles split or join without a pop (test/seaGrid.test.ts checks both). `seaWaves.ts` hands the vertex stage `seaAt`'s waves as one weighted list with each phase worked out in double precision near the eye (`forEachShownSet` in `sea.ts` is the one place that lists the sets showing), so the drawn surface is `seaAt`'s to a millimetre 60 km out and hours into a match. A wave is drawn fully where it spans 6 cells of the grid and fades out by 3 (`SEA_WAVE_FULL_CELLS`, `SEA_WAVE_GONE_CELLS`): far waves fade into a flat sea instead of flickering, and near the eye waves shorter than about 1.5 m are left to the fine sea (N1.2), which at forces 1 and 2 includes the simulated set's shortest waves. The earth's curve (`earth.ts#earthDrop`) bends the drawn sea down around the eye, so the horizon is a true one (3.57 √h km off) and a clean line. For N1.4: `seaMaterial.ts` has a plain stand-in look (sky colour by Fresnel, a sun glint, a dark body, haze toward the horizon's colour, all from `SkyView.light`) to replace; the vertex stage passes the surface normal (`vSeaNormal`) and the vector to the eye (`vSeaToEye`), and the glint shows the grid's facets where it is sharp, so N1.4's normals should come per pixel. For N1.8: the knobs are `SEA_TILE_CELLS`, `SEA_FINEST_CELL` and `SEA_LOD_RANGE` in `seaGrid.ts`; at the defaults the camera sees 200 to 330 tiles of 289 vertices, each summing the 28 waves (53 during a change of wind). For N2.5 and N3.8: everything drawn far off must sink by `earthDrop` like the sea, or it floats; the sea's mesh has its own origin, stepping by 1024 m with the eye. `?eye=` (metres, 0.5 to 200) and `?wind=` (Beaufort force 0 to 8) now set the scene (`src/app/params.ts`); N1.9 adds to them. Checked headless with WebGL 2 (`?gl`) at 3 m, 10 m and 30 m, looking level, down and across, in a breeze and a gale; on WebGPU the sea's shader was compiled headless without a message, but no frame was drawn: the cloud's software WebGPU loses its device before the first frame, on `main` before this package too, so WebGPU was checked on Arnd's PC instead (Chrome, RTX 5070): 16.7 ms a frame (vsync) at 3, 4.5 and 30 m and in a gale, the shader built in the warm-up with no compile later, no holes below the horizon, and the horizon a clean line within one pixel row; the iPad look is still to come.

N1.4 (the sea's material) is `seaMaterial.ts`, with its parts beside it. Per pixel the normal sums every simulated wave and the fine waves (`seaFine.ts`: 28 seeded waves per wind layer from just below the simulated set's shortest down to 0.5 m, faded in and out with their layer), each faded out by the pixel's footprint (`PIXEL_FULL`, `PIXEL_GONE`), and below 0.5 m the ripples (`seaRipples.ts`: a tiling texture of 96 ripples down to 3.5 cm, laid twice at two sizes turned a quarter to each other, drifting their own ways; single waves that short drew a regular weave under the eye). How rough the sea is comes from Cox and Munk's mean square slope for the wind (glassy below 0.6 m/s); what the simulated waves carry is taken off, the fine waves and ripples carry the rest, and whatever a pixel cannot draw becomes roughness (LEAN-like), so the sky's reflection blurs and the sun's and moon's glitter (Beckmann facets) widens with distance and wind instead of flickering. The sky is the rig's environment map (`fullSky`, Karis's fit for its strength); the water's body is a deep blue lit by the sun and the sky, greener where foam has filled it and greyer when stirred in a gale; light shows through the thin tops of crests toward the sun; haze toward the horizon's colour. Foam forms where the waves bunch (the vertex stage's stretch, now and 1.6 s and 4 s ago at the same water, so it lingers and fades); the level that turns white is set each moment so the share of white water is Monahan's whitecap share for the wind (`seaFoam.ts#foamLevels`), broken up by a seeded pattern (fresh foam with bubbles, old foam thinning to winding strands). Gusts are a 128² map around the eye (`seaGusts.ts`) that roughens the fine waves (a light air's gust shows dark on glassy water). From below the sea shows Snell's window, total reflection outside it, and murk with distance (the 'deep water' kind, a sphere around the eye when it is under the surface). On WebGPU, building the sea's shader in the warm-up left the sky's environment map blank until the rig drew it again, which with the clock stopped never came: `SkyRig.redrawEnvironment()` (AssetGenerator) is called once after the warm-up. `?yaw=` and `?pitch=` (degrees) aim the view and `?eye=` goes down to −40 m for views from under water. Checked on Arnd's PC (WebGPU, RTX 5070): 16.7 ms a frame (vsync) at dawn, noon, dusk and night, calm, breeze and gale, and under water, ten shaders in the warm-up and none later, no console errors; the iPad's cost is still to measure. For N1.8: the knobs are `SEA_FINE_PER_LAYER` (each fine wave is a sine per pixel near the eye), `PIXEL_FULL`/`PIXEL_GONE` and the ripples' two texture reads. For N2.5: foam and wake enter through the material's foam (`vSeaFoam`: fresh and old foam per vertex) and the body's aerated colour; far off, foam on the coarse tiles is still a soft-edged sheet. For N1.9: wave heights, swell and gusts were judged by eye here and left at their values; `?day=0` stops the clock but not the sea, so standard shots need a fixed sea time.

N1.7 (sound of sea and wind) is in `src/audio/`: `ambience.ts` decides the beds, `slaps.ts` the sounds placed near the listener, `tuning.ts` holds every number, and `seaSound.ts` plays them through `@futuremagic/audio`'s `AudioEngine` (main.ts calls `sound.hear(...)` each frame with the camera's ear, facing and crown, and `ship.below`). The sea's beds (calm, breeze, strong breeze, gale) crossfade at equal power by the force of the sea (`seaForce`: each wave layer's wind as a fractional Beaufort force, `beaufortForce`, weighted by its energy share), so the sound changes over the same 3 minutes as the waves and never ahead of them; the wind's beds (light, fresh, strong, gale) by `windAt`'s speed at the ear, gusts included, gliding in 0.6 s; a deep swell surge rises with the water's vertical speed under the ear. Every bed is two 30 s takes blended by a slow seeded swing (47 to 113 s, each bed its own), on top of the engine's two chains at rates 1 and 0.97, so the mix does not come round within minutes. Eight points on the water 3 to 11 m around the ear are watched; when a crest passes one (the water there stops rising, above 0.6 of the sea's RMS height) a seeded share starts a sound there: laps in a calm, hull slaps from force 2, crests breaking into foam from force 3, heavy seas bursting from force 5.5, about one a second in a breeze and never closer than 0.3 s. Under water (`?eye=-3`) the world is heard through a 350 Hz low-pass, below deck through 900 Hz until N2.8 makes below deck sound right. Sound starts on the first tap: the engine (AssetGenerator #185) now listens to every press for the page's life, since Safari accepts only a tap's end or a click and suspends the context in the background, and plays a silent sample in the press; Safari's audio session is set to `playback`, so the sound is heard with the device set to silent. The takes are in `public/sound/` (4.5 MB in all, 64 kbit/s mono at 32 kHz, fetched after the first tap and only what the weather asks for, about 2 MB at one force); their prompts, how they were made and checked are in `tools/sound/` (README and sounds.json; raw takes in the project's shared folder). Decoded, a bed is 3.8 MB and nine play between two forces, so the engine keeps up to 48 MB (`SOUND_MEMORY`). Checked headless: the first click and the first tap open the sound, the beds load and play at the levels above, slaps start. Not checked: how it sounds. No listening model is reliable (one heard "surf on a beach" in most sea takes, the first round's were); Arnd judges by ear at G1, and any take is replaced with `make.mjs --again <id>`. For N2.8: the ship's own sounds (timber, water along the hull, below deck) go beside these, through the same engine (`SeaSound.engine`); the muffle below deck is a stand-in to replace. For N3.9: wind in the rigging follows the apparent wind; these wind beds are the true wind on the ear and should duck under it. For N7.3: the sound level is `engine.setVolume('master' | 'world', v)`.

N1.8 (quality levels) is `src/app/quality.ts`: `QUALITY_LEVELS` (high, medium, low, lowest) is the one table of knobs, and `QualityChooser` picks a level from measured frame time. Every device starts at high and steps down while too many frames are late, so nothing tells tablet from desktop by the browser's name (Safari on the iPad says Mac). After the first second it measures a level for 1.5 s of frames (after 0.5 s to settle); a frame later than 25 ms (one and a half frames at 60 fps, so a display's vsync showed the frame before twice) is late, and a level holds while at most 5 % of its frames are late. It never steps up: with vsync the frame time does not show how much room is left, and a GPU timer would need the Stage's renderer to ask for `timestamp-query` (AssetGenerator). A hidden page drops what it had measured of the level (`interrupt`). The level and why show on the HUD's second line (`?hud`), with the pixels drawn; `?quality=high|medium|low|lowest` fixes a level and measures nothing (for shots and the bench). The knobs are only those that change without a compile, as the level changes while the game runs: `renderScale` (the share of the device's pixels along each side, on top of the Stage's cap of 2 per CSS pixel: 1, 0.8, 0.65, 0.5, so lowest draws the iPad's screen at one pixel per CSS pixel), `seaCells` (16 or 8 cells a sea tile: the tile keeps the densest grid's vertices and the index holds a run of triangles for every density, chosen by the draw range, and the shader's `spacing` uniform makes the sparse grid's odd vertices slide onto the coarser grid as the dense grid's do, so levels still meet without cracks) and, for N1.2, `fineSeaBands` and `fineSeaSize` (read by nothing yet). N1.2 (the fine sea on the GPU) is `seaFine.ts` (pure: the spectrum laid out in bands) and `seaBands.ts` (the GPU), replacing N1.4's 28 per-pixel fine waves per layer. The short waves from 48 m down to 0.5 m (the ripples' texture still takes over below) are one spectrum, the tail of the simulated set's (height density ∝ k⁻³, an equal slope per octave), its level set so that they carry Cox and Munk's slope less what the simulated waves carry, exactly the budget N1.4's waves had; they begin at 0.9 of the set's shortest wave and fade in over a third of an octave, with a cos-2s spread from s = 2 at 48 m to 0.5 at 0.5 m. That range is split into bands equal on a log scale: at `high` three of 256², 48 to 10.5 m, 10.5 to 2.3 m and 2.3 to 0.5 m, on tiles of 384, 79 and 16 m (a tile holds 8 of its longest waves, or fewer where its shortest would span under 4 texels, and the tiles are nudged 6 % apart so no two share a period). Each frame one compute pass builds every row's spectrum of the moment (the two strongest wind layers, weighted in energy, so a change of wind neither jumps nor swells) and transforms it, then one pass per band transforms its columns (Stockham's radix-2 FFT in workgroup memory, one workgroup per line: four dispatches in all, where a pass per stage would take 16 per band) and writes two rgba16float textures per band: `shape` (the water's sideways shift and height) and `slope` (slope along x and z, the bunching (the shift's divergence), and the foam a fold leaves, lingering e^(−t / 4 s)). Frequencies sit on whole multiples of 2π / 4096 s, so the GPU's clock wraps without losing precision. Only `slope` has mipmaps (three makes them each frame, one small render pass per level); a stage may write only four storage textures, hence a column pass per band. In the material every band's slope adds to the normal (gusts grow the short bands more), the part of a band too fine for the pixel is roughness (its range's share, on a log scale, below the pixel's `PIXEL_GONE` length), and the vertex stage adds the bands' shape where the grid holds them (fully where a band's shortest wave spans 3 cells, gone where its middle one no longer does). Foam where waves fold: fresh foam is now made per pixel from the simulated waves' stretch (`vSeaFoam.x` now carries the raw stretch) plus the bands' bunching, white where it lies `foamDepth` deviations below the mean of what the pixel draws, so the white share stays Monahan's at every distance and a crest breaks in ragged patches where the short waves crowd on it; where the bands are too fine for the pixel the broad noise breaks it up as before. Old foam is still the vertex stage's lagged stretch, its lace now partly the bands' fold foam. The quality knobs (N1.8's `fineSeaBands`, `fineSeaSize`) change nothing compiled: every size the levels name gets a band set at the start (`high` to `low` share the 256² set, `lowest` has 128²), all compiled in the warm-up (`FineSea.warmUp`, `compileComputeAsync`); the material reads as many bands as the most any level asks for, switched by a uniform. WebGPU is required: `createRenderer` asks for `webgpu` only, a browser without it gets one plain sentence, and `?gl` is gone. Headless: Chromium's software GPU renders WebGPU but cannot present it to a canvas (the device lost before the first frame in N1.3's note was this), so `npm run shot` injects a stand-in swap chain (`tools/shot/swapchain.js`) and paints the last frame for the picture; CI's browser check runs on WebGPU now, and shot also prints three's warnings (a shader node that fails to build is only a warning, and its mesh is not drawn). Checked in the cloud on that software GPU: the band textures read back carry each band's slope to 5 %; shots in calm, breeze and gale, at 3, 4.5 and 30 m and at `lowest`, no errors, 15 shaders in the warm-up and none later. Not verified on a real GPU tonight: the iPad's cost (the estimate is four compute dispatches over 3 × 256² and 24 tiny mipmap passes a frame; the probe's FFT-shaped chain took 0.125 ms there), its look on the iPad, and whether a band's tile shows from deck height on a real display. Reading the 1 ms off the iPad: `?bench` (N1.9) times whole frames, not passes, so it shows the bands' cost only as part of the frame; if the frame holds its refresh at `high` with room to spare, the bands fit, and their own time needs the per-pass GPU timing N1.9 left for later (three's `trackTimestamp` in `@futuremagic/stage`). The bands run in `seaView.fine.run(renderer)` before the frame is drawn; their mipmaps are made while the sea is drawn. The headless shots also showed N2.4's damage map uploaded empty (its byte count read before it was set, so the bundle made a map of no bytes, a WebGPU validation error on every page); fixed here in `shipMaterials.ts`. For N2.5: wake foam can join the per-pixel `fresh` in the material; the bands' shape is not in `surfaceAt` (a few centimetres at most).

**Design decisions for N1 (design thread, 2026-11-17; Arnd may overrule at G1):** a flat calm keeps a swell from elsewhere (N1.10), because a mirror-still sea looks dead and a ship should always breathe under your feet. A duel is fought in force 2 to 6: below that the ships do not move, above it the guns cannot be worked; force 0 and the gale exist for looking at the sea. Wave heights and periods stay at the open-sea values until they are judged by eye at N1.4, then the rule table is tuned once, there.

**Open design questions for N1 (design thread):** the Beaufort presets a duel may have (calm to near gale: a full gale makes gunnery impossible and may be for later); whether weather changes during a match.

**Design decisions for N3 (design thread, 2026-11-18; Arnd judges the feel at G3 and may overrule):**

- **Pace.** N3.3's first proposal stands as built: the speed table (plain sail, force 5: no way within 60° of the wind, 5 knots close-hauled, 10 on the beam, 11 to 11.5 on the quarter, 10 running), turns about three times as tight as a real brig's, a tack at speed through the wind in 20 to 55 s, stays missed from about 1 knot with about two and a half minutes to gather way again. Nothing here is tuned further before Arnd has sailed her at G3; what he judges there is whether closing takes too long, whether a tack feels like a risk worth taking, and whether a missed tack punishes too hard.
- **Heaving to stops her.** "Heave to" is one order that does all of it: the main yards laid aback, the courses hauled up, the boom sail brailed, the helm put down. She loses her way within about a minute in a fresh breeze (force 5 and up) and lies still enough to fight or wait; in light winds the heavy hull coasts longer (about 100 s in force 4, 150 s in force 3, over four minutes in force 2, as N3.4 measured). That is left as it is for Arnd to feel at G3. Backing a mast alone stays possible as its own order.
- **Light airs are not slow (2026-11-18, changes the line above for light winds only).** Found in play: close-hauled in force 3 she made about 2 knots after a tack, which feels like stopping, and Arnd's rule of the day is fun before accuracy. So the wind's force changes the sea, the heel, the sound and the risk far more than her speed. Targets, settled, in force 3: under plain sail 4 knots close-hauled, 7 on the beam, 8 on the quarter; under battle sail never under 3 knots close-hauled. In force 2 no less than three quarters of that. After a tack at speed in force 3 and 4 she is back at 3 knots within 30 s of filling on the new tack; from force 5 up the recovery stays as built (about 50 s at force 5, longer in a gale), which is the tack's price in a fresh wind and is judged at G3. The force 5 table and everything above it stay as they are, and a missed tack still costs its two and a half minutes. Changed in `src/sim/rules/sailing.ts` and nowhere else, as a fix (N3.3's owner file); `check:sailing` gets the force 3 rows, and `check:captains` and the balance sheet are run again after it.

- **A course she cannot lie is refused in words, not obeyed into irons.** Ordered to steer closer than close-hauled, the master answers that she will not lie that course (an event with the reason) and steers close-hauled on the nearer tack. Tacking is the order that takes her through the wind: a course order never does. A course on the other tack is reached by wearing, and a course she cannot lie that falls on the other tack ends close-hauled on that tack, by wearing; a course in the wind's eye itself keeps the tack she is on. Whenever a course order makes her wear, the master says so (an event, "Wearing round, sir"), so the captain is never surprised by the long way round and can order a tack instead.
- **The start.** (The distance is now set by time, see the balance sheet under N7: about three minutes of closing.) The two ships start 1,500 m apart with the wind on the beam of the line between them, each under battle sail and steering to close, so neither has the weather position and the first decision is who works for it. The match's seed turns the whole picture round the compass and picks the force (2 to 6). Other starts (a chase, one ship to windward) come with the match settings in N7.

- **A sail's state is one number the crew moves** (2026-11-18, found by N3.10). How far each sail is set is a share from furled to sheeted home, held in the ship's state and advanced by the crew's work on it; the wind's force on the sail grows with it, and the drawing shows exactly that share, with no timer of its own. So what the captain sees being set is what is already driving her, on every machine.
- **Every sail she carries draws on every course the master accepts** (2026-11-18, found by N3.10: full and by left square sails aback and shivering). "Full and by" means as close as she lies with every set sail full: the helmsman steers by the sails, not by a fixed angle. A sail shivers or lies aback only while she turns through the wind, when a mast is backed by order, or when she is caught by a shift, and then the master says so. `check:sailing` holds this for every wind and canvas.

- **The state** is `SimState { matchSeed, tick, wind, sea, ships }`, plain data with no class, closure or typed array in it. The clock is `tick`, a whole count of steps; `simTime(state)` is `tick / 30`, worked out, never summed, so every machine has the same time to the bit. `startMatch({ seed, windForce, windFrom, ships: [{ x, z, heading }] })` makes the wind and the sea from seeds derived from the match's seed (`'wind'`, `'sea'`) and puts each ship still on her line. A ship is `{ motion, course }`, known by her place in `ships`; how the two start (distance, who has the wind) is still the caller's, an open question above.
- **A step** (`step(state, orders) -> { state, events }`): the orders in the order given (the host's list is the truth: two orders for one ship in a step, the later stands), then the sea follows the wind's mean at the step's start (`followWind`), then each ship floats one `stepMotion` on that sea under her `course`. It never changes the state it is given.
- **Orders for now:** one kind, `{ kind: 'course', ship, heading, speed }`, taken at once (the heading wrapped, the speed 0 to `TOP_SPEED`, 12 knots, in rules/ships.ts). An order that cannot be taken gives the event `{ kind: 'refused', tick, order, reason }`. `course` is owned by step.ts as a stand-in: **for N3.3**, the sailing model takes over heading and speed (rules.json's `course` row moves to sailing.ts or goes), and its forces (sail heel, damping) go through motion.ts, which owns the pose; **for N3.4**, the vocabulary moves to orders.ts and replaces `CourseOrder`, keeping the `refused` event's shape.
- **Bytes and hash** (`codec.ts`): one schema-less codec for state, orders and events, so a package adding a field to `SimState` touches nothing here. Keys are written sorted (canonical bytes whatever order fields were made in), whole 32-bit numbers as zigzag varints, every other number as its 8 IEEE bytes (-0 and NaN exact). It refuses anything not plain data, so no state can hide in a typed array or a class. `hashState` is 64 bits as 16 hex digits. A duel's state is about 3.7 KB, nearly all the sea's wave sets (25 waves a set, six numbers each, two or three sets): **for N3.7**, a state a second fits the 16 KB budget as it is; sending the sea only when `setsMade` changes would bring a state to a few hundred bytes. Encoding and hashing a state takes about 80 µs, about a step's cost.
- **Running on through orders:** `replay(state, log, untilTick, each)` with `log = [{ tick, orders }]`, what a guest does with the host's orders (N3.7) and what a saved match replays.
- **Cost:** a step of two ships is about 110 µs on the cloud machine (about 4 500 sine and cosine pairs); the test holds it under a quarter of the 2 ms budget by `pace`. Ten minutes of a duel is 18 000 steps, about 2.3 s, so the ten-minute check is a slow check (`npm run check:replay`, in CI's slow job): two runs in separate processes and one through bytes at five minutes agree at every simulated second, in the strongest wind, with the sea taking new wave sets on the way. Within one engine the state is bit-exact; across engines (the iPad's Safari and a PC's Chrome) `Math.sin`, `atan` and the like may differ in the last bit, so **N3.7** should compare hashes as a check and let the host's state win, not expect guests stepping alone to stay equal forever.

**Notes:** N3.3 (sailing model), `src/sim/ship/sailing.ts`, its numbers in `src/sim/rules/sailing.ts`.
- **Her state** is `SimShip.sailing` (owner sailing.ts, rules.json row `sailing`; the `course` stand-in is gone): `heading` and `turnRate`, `ahead` (way, negative is sternway) and `leeway` (m/s to starboard), `helm` (rudder angle), `steer` (what the helmsman steers: a heading and `turn` +1 starboard, -1 larboard, 0 the short way), `canvas` (each sail's `{ set, reefs }` in `rigPlan().sails` order), and per mast (`MASTS`: fore, main) `braces` (share of the brace limit, + braced for a wind from starboard), `sides` (which side each mast's sails take the wind on) and `backed` (yards laid aback). `stepSailing(sailing, pose, windAt(...))` gives the next state, `Way { heading, ahead, leeway, rollMoment }` for `stepMotion` (which owns the pose: it now moves her by `ahead` and `leeway` and adds the sails' heeling moment to her roll), and events. The pose's `heading` is the copy motion takes from the way.
- **How it works:** each set sail presses by its angle of attack to the apparent wind (true wind with gusts, less her way, leeway and turning) through a lift and drag polar (square and fore-and-aft), keyed by the sine of the angle, so no arc function runs (same bits on every engine, apart from `windAt` itself). The crew trims each mast's yards for the best drive (11 tries across the brace limits of N3.2, swinging at `BRACE_RATE`) and each fore-and-aft sheet the same way. A mast's sails change sides only when the wind is past the bow by `HAUL` (main 10°, fore 40°: so in a tack the fore yards stay aback and push her round) or past the stern by `GYBE`, and only toward the side the helmsman is bound for. Hull: resistance ahead (with a steep wave term near her hull speed), sideways (leeway 5 to 6° close-hauled) and to turning; the rudder by the square of her speed (no way, no steering; sternway reverses it, and the helmsman shifts his helm). Her roll swings each sail across the wind and the canvas presses against it (`SAIL_ROLL_SLOPE`): under battle sail in a force 5 beam sea her roll swings about half as far as under bare poles (RMS 2.6° against 5.2°, most 11° against 19°).
- **Pace (tuned, not measured):** her turns are about three times as tight as a real brig's, and `TURN_SKID` scales down the way she would lose skidding through them. A tack ordered at speed is through the wind in 20 to 55 s (quicker the stronger the wind) and settled on the new tack within a minute; from 1 knot she misses stays (`missed stays` event: she lost her way or began to fall back while turning through the wind's eye), the helmsman bears away onto the old tack and she falls back with sternway, then gathers way, about two and a half minutes in all. Wearing from close-hauled gybes the boom sail across after about 30 s. The speed table (`SPEED_TABLE`, plain sail, force 5): no way within 60° (55°: aback, she goes astern), 2.5 kn at 60°, 5 close-hauled (67.5°), 10.2 on the beam, 11.5 at 110°, 11 at 135°, 10 before the wind; through the sea and gusts (`npm run check:sailing`) she is fastest at 135° and close-hauled is about 4 kn. Light winds are slow (force 3 battle sail: 3 to 4.5 kn), force 6 and up runs at 10 to 13 kn. This answers "how much pace" only as a first proposal for the design thread and Arnd at G3.
- **Canvas** (`CANVAS`): bare, battle (topsails, jib, fore topmast staysail, boom sail), plain (adds courses and topgallants), all. The crew sets it for the match's force (`CARRIED_UP_TO`, `REEF_AT`): in a gale every setting is close-reefed topsails, the fore topmast staysail and a reefed boom sail. Steady heel stays under `HEEL_LIMIT` (15°, the lee port sills 1.76 m over the water) in every preset: about 9° in the gale.
- **For N3.4:** the stand-in orders in step.ts (`course` with `turn`, `canvas`, `back` with a mast), each taken at once, are what the vocabulary builds on: a tack is a course on the other tack turning toward the wind, a wear the same turning away; `steerTo`, `setCanvas`, `layAback` are the sailing model's own calls. Heaving to is the main laid aback: she slows over two to three minutes, but her boom sail and headsails keep her forereaching, so "heave to" should also take in or back canvas. The events `tacked`, `wore`, `missed stays` come out of `step` as `{ kind, tick, ship }`. Nothing refuses an order to steer into the wind's eye: she is taken aback and goes astern, so N3.4 should refuse or warn.
- **For N3.9 and N3.10:** `sailsStanding(sailing, wind)` gives each sail's area, brace or sheet angle (+ starboard), the wind's pressure on it, whether it is aback and whether it shivers.
- **Cost:** a step under sail is under 1000 sine and cosine pairs (about 7 µs on the cloud machine); a duel step of two ships is still well within its quarter of the 2 ms budget.

**Notes:** N3.4 (orders), `src/sim/orders.ts`, its times and judgements in `src/sim/rules/orders.ts`. The vocabulary is the sailing rows of [orders.md](orders.md).
- **The orders** (`Order`, plain data, `VOCABULARY` lists each with who takes it and what it does): `steer` {heading}, `up`, `away` (a point, 11.25°, from the course she has been told, so repeated taps add up), `by` (full and by), `tack`, `wear`, `heave`, `sail` {canvas: battle, plain, all}, `shorten` (one step down to bare poles), `back` {mast}, `fill`. `parseOrder(text, ship)` reads them from words ("steer 090", "course nne", "come up a point, sir", "back the main topsail", "take in sail") or says why the words are none; `orderWords` writes them back. `malformed` checks an untrusted order from another machine.
- **What she has been told** is `SimShip.told` (owner orders.ts, rules.json row `told`): `helm` (a course, or full and by on a tack), `canvas`, `hove`, `backed` per mast, `acts` (the timed work still to do) and `given` (the orders still being carried out, each with its id and tick). `giveOrder` answers at once: taken, refused (`told` unchanged), or answered and changed (a course she will not lie). The `refused` event keeps its shape `{ kind, tick, order, reason }`; an order taken with words raises `{ kind: 'answered', tick, ship, order, code, reason }` instead (orders.md, "Answers are events"), its `code` (`AnswerCode` in orders.ts: `will not lie`, `wearing round`) for the phrase book (N4.5) and its reason in English for the order panel. A new event `{ kind: 'done', tick, ship, order }` comes when an order's last act is done.
- **Times** (until the crew exists; gone since N4.1, whose men now give each order its time: see the N4 notes): helm 2 s, tack 6 s to "helm's a-lee", wear 4 s, a mast's yards 12 s; sails one after another per gang (each mast's square sails, the headsails, the after sails), the gangs side by side, 4 s to start, then `SAIL_SECONDS` per sail (topsail 20, course and topgallant 14, boom sail 8, headsails and staysails 6): plain from battle about 32 s. A later order for the same helm, sail or mast replaces the work not yet done; any sail order replaces all pending sail work. **For N4.1:** each act (`HelmAct`, `SailAct`, `YardsAct`, with `of`, the order's id) is a task; replace the times by the men's work and keep the acts as the seam into the sailing model (`steerTo`, `setSail`, `layAback`). `setCanvas` is gone; `setSail` sets one sail.
- **Rules the master keeps:** a course never takes her through the wind's eye; a course on the other tack is reached by wearing (the `wore` event), tacking is its own order. Closer than close-hauled (less than `CLOSE_HAULED` less a degree off the wind) he answers "she will not lie 030: full and by on the larboard tack, 068" and steers full and by on the tack the ordered course lies on (on the other tack: "wearing round"). Tack and wear are refused without way (`NO_WAY`, half a knot: "she has no way on her", which covers in irons and hove to); a tack while she is in stays (ordered and not yet at the helm, or turning up toward the other tack) is refused until she is round. Full and by, the helmsman follows the mean wind as it shifts (each degree). When she misses stays the master's bearing away becomes her course (`heard`). Decisions use the mean wind (`meanWind(...).from`), which swings but has no gusts.
- **Heaving to.** As ruled, one order: the courses hauled up, the boom sail brailed, the helm down (full and by), and also the headsails and staysails hauled down and **both** topsails laid aback until her way is off; then the fore yards fill and the crew works the main yards (aback with headway, filled with sternway) so she lies within half a knot of still, drifting to leeward under a knot. The main alone left her forereaching for minutes, and even both topsails aback cannot stop 440 t faster than the canvas presses: from a beam reach under battle sail her way is off after 51 to 70 s in force 5 to 8, 100 s in force 4, 150 s in force 3 and over four minutes in force 2 (`npm run check:sailing` prints it). "Within about a minute" holds from force 5 up; faster in light winds would mean more hull resistance (rules/sailing.ts `HULL_AHEAD`), a question of pace for the design thread and G3. "Fill" brings her out: every mast filled, her canvas set again as ordered, full and by.
- **For N3.5:** give orders through `step` (or `parseOrder` from typed words), show `refused` reasons and `done` events; `told.given` is "what did I tell him", `VOCABULARY` the buttons. The match start of the N3 design decisions (1,500 m, wind on the beam) is not built here: `startMatch` still takes the ships' places from its caller.
- **For N3.7:** orders are plain data and small; a guest sends `Order`s, the host checks them with `giveOrder` (malformed ones are refused, never thrown). `told` adds a few hundred bytes to a ship's state while orders are under way.

**Notes:** N3.5 (typed orders and buttons), `?sail`: `src/app/orderPanel.ts` (the panel on the page), `src/app/orderReport.ts` (its words, pure), `src/app/soloMatch.ts` (the match stepped on this machine alone, pure).
- **What it is.** The brig under way in a match of her own (`startMatch` as it is: one ship where the ship view lays her, heading north, 5 knots, battle sail; wind from `WIND_FROM` at `?wind=`'s force; since N3.7 the duel's start, its wind's force the address's when `?wind=` or `?sea=` names one), the match's wind and sea driving the page, her pose drawn from the match between steps (`ShipView.poseFrom`), the off-ship standpoints keeping their place beside her. The panel (top right, folds to its title): her heading, way, the mean wind and her point of sail, canvas, hove to and masts aback, what the crew is doing for each order (since N4.1 from `crew.tasks`: the act and job in hand, its men waiting, on the way or at work, the seconds of work left, the jobs after it), the last six answers (refused, the master's answers, done, short-handed, tacked, wore, missed stays); a compass row of eight points for courses, every other order of `VOCABULARY` as a button (master, first lieutenant), a typed field read by `parseOrder`. Since N3.6 her yards, boom and gaff are drawn braced as the sailing model trims them (`sparAngles`, now pageMatch.ts, into `ShipView.setBrace`), so a tack is seen aloft as the yards swing round; since N3.10 the sails too. With that, the panel and the sea going by are how she answers.
- **Wearing round.** As the design thread ruled, a course order that makes her wear answers "wearing round, sir: 160 lies on the other tack" (orders.ts). Like the answered course it comes as an `answered` event (code `wearing round`) while the order is taken; refusals stay `refused` (2026-11-18, after N3.7).
- **For N3.7:** the panel talks to the match only through `OrderSession` (orderReport.ts: `ship`, `state`, `give`, `on('events')`, `state` being the state after the step whose events are handed on), agreed with the N3.7 thread: its session takes the place of `SoloMatch`, which can then go, and `?sail` points at the offline session. Keep `sparAngles` (move it beside the session if soloMatch.ts goes). `SoloMatch` gives one order a step and catches up at most ten steps a call.
- **For N4.4:** the panel stays as a test tool (orders.md); the fan replaces it for play. `wouldRefuse` and `isStanding` are what the fan's dim and ringed orders need.

Sailing takes her kilometres from where she started: her shaders read no world position and the sea's origin follows the eye, so nothing should lose precision, but a long sail on the iPad is the check.

A room's seed is its code's hash, so both machines know it. `startMatch` still takes any setup (tests, N7's other starts).
- **The host steps; guests run the match themselves.** The host steps by its own clock and gives each step the orders that came in for it. Every 3 steps it sends a delta (the orders by tick, the captains, the last order of each captain stepped), with the state's hash once a second; the whole state (codec bytes in base64, about 6 KB for an idle duel) every 5 s, for late joiners and a change of host (GameHub replays the latest state and the deltas after it), and at once when a guest asks. A guest runs on through the host's orders 5 steps behind the host's clock (its idea of that clock follows the deltas), so it makes every step itself and gets the same events (refused, done, tacked) for the panel; at each hash it compares, and on a difference it asks for the host's state and takes it (`drifts` counts them; the HUD shows them). - **Orders on the wire** are JSON. The host passes every order it steps, its own too, through `cleanOrder` (only an order's fields, named to the sender's ship, through JSON), because `wrapBearing(-0)` keeps -0 and JSON would not; anything malformed still becomes an order that the step refuses with its reason, never a throw. A captain numbers his orders; the host steps each number once (`acks`), so orders sent again after a change of host are not stepped twice.
- **The host leaving.** GameHub makes the lowest id host. It runs on through every delta the old host sent (nothing it passed on is lost), frees the ship of whoever left, puts its own orders no host has stepped into its first step and sends its state; the others send it their unstepped orders again. The steps the old host made after its last delta are made again by the new one, from the same orders.
- **Network budget** (restated by the design thread, 2026-11-18, to how this is built): every machine steps the match itself; the host sends about 10 deltas a second (the orders since the last one, a hash once a second; 62 bytes on average, 184 at most with two crews making sail) and a whole state every 5 s, on a join and on a hash mismatch (about 15.6 KB as sent with two crews at work, the codec's bytes in base64). `test/duelHost.test.ts` holds the budget: at most 20 deltas a second under 1 KB each, a whole state under 64 KB.
- **Not done here:** warming the crew's routes at load (N4.1's note): the fields are kept per JavaScript realm, so warming them in the build worker would not help the page's thread, which steps the match; N4.3 should warm them on the page behind the loading screen (or run the simulation in a worker). The fine sea's small waves are seeded from the page's first match, the same on every machine, not from the room's.
- **Checks.** `test/duel.test.ts`, `test/duelHost.test.ts` (the fake hub, `test/support/fakeHub.ts`, keeps the server's rules and sends every message through JSON). `npm run check:room`: two headless browsers on `?netcheck` play one match through `tools/net/standin.mjs` (GameHub's wire protocol on this machine; the cloud container cannot reach the live server), about 15 s; with `GAMEHUB_URL=https://games.futuremagic.de` against the live server. **Faces (Arnd, 2026-11-18: "we do have facial expressions in actors. These should be used when people are displayed."). A standing rule for every package that shows people.** Actors gives a full character nine expression presets blended from 34 face units, a living face (blinks, small eye movements, eyes that follow a point, idle movement of the face) and mouth shapes from text. Nauta uses all of it:

- **Every full character has a living face**, always: he blinks, his eyes move, and when the captain speaks to him or he reports, he looks at the captain.
- **His expression follows what he is doing and what has just happened to him**, from the simulation's tasks and events, never from a mood number (the first game has none): strain when hauling, heaving at the capstan or running out a gun; narrowed eyes aloft and in spray; a flinch when shot passes close; fear and shock just after his ship is hit near him, fading over some seconds into a set, grim face while the fight lasts; pain when wounded; slack when dead; a shout when he calls an order down the chain; relief and joy when she strikes. The stronger feeling wins and each fades in and out, never switches.
- **A speaker's mouth moves** while his speech bubble shows, shaped from the bubble's own words. There is still no voice.
- **One place decides it:** a table from task and event to expression, its strength and how long it lasts, read by everything that draws a face (N4.3 the crew, N4.5 the bubbles, N5.4b the gun crews, N6.6 the men under fire, N7.2 and N7.7 if a face is shown there).
- **Crowd figures** are too small and too many for a living face. They carry feeling in the body (the flinch, the crouch, the stagger). Fixed faces on them (calm, straining, shouting, in pain) were tried in X5 and dropped: at 10 m only a shout shows, at 15 m and beyond none does. A man who matters at that moment (speaking, struck, addressed) is brought into the pool of full characters, so the face the captain looks at is always a living one.
- The violence setting does not touch faces: fear and pain are shown at every level, wounds are what it changes.

**Design decisions for N5 (design thread, 2026-11-18; first proposals, the numbers live in `src/sim/rules/guns.ts` and are tuned at G5 and in N7.5):**

- **Who aims.** The duel has one enemy, so she is the target. The captain orders what to fire (round, chain, grape), at what part of her (hull, rigging, deck) and when (as they bear, or on his word); see [orders.md](orders.md). Every gun captain trains and elevates his own gun onto that part without being told. The captain never lays a gun.
- **The roll is the skill.** A gun's shot leaves along the barrel as the ship lies at that instant. "As they bear" lets each gun captain pick his own moment of the roll, with a seeded error that grows with the sea and shrinks with a full, unhurt crew. "On my word" fires every loaded gun of the side at the captain's instant: on the up-roll high into her rigging, on the down-roll low into her hull or the sea. That choice is the captain's and it matters.
- **The guns.** 32-pounder carronade: muzzle speed about 230 m/s, trains 30° either way, elevates from 5° down to 11° up, reloads in 35 s with its full crew. Long 6-pounder: about 480 m/s, trains 20° either way, the same elevation, reloads in 30 s. Short-handed, the reload stretches in proportion to the hands missing, and a gun with fewer than two men stops.
- **Range and accuracy, for pace.** Against a brig lying broadside on in a force 4 sea, with guns fired as they bear: at 100 m about three round shot in four strike her, at 200 m about half, at 400 m about one in five; beyond 600 m only the long sixes are worth firing. Chain is for her rigging out to about 250 m; grape is for her men inside about 100 m. So the fight is decided inside 200 m, closing is a choice with a cost, and the long sixes give the chase its sting.
- **Left out.** Powder and shot are not counted in the first game; no double-shotting; no misfires or burst guns.

- **Smoke and the frame's edges** (2026-11-18). Half-resolution smoke needs the scene's depth, and the frame is drawn straight to the canvas with 4x multisampling, whose depth cannot be read. The clean edges of masts, yards and rigging against the sky are the first of the details that make her feel real, so multisampling is not given up for the smoke without Arnd having seen the two side by side on the iPad. The order of preference: (1) keep multisampling and get a depth the smoke can read (the scene drawn into a multisampled target of the shared post chain, its depth brought down to half resolution in one small pass), if the renderer allows it at a cost inside the smoke's 2 ms; (2) failing that, the post chain with a post-process edge filter, only after Arnd has compared rigging and horizon both ways; (3) until one of these is in, smoke is drawn at full resolution in the main pass with its density as the quality knob. A depth of the far hull alone is not acceptable: her own sails must hide the smoke behind them.

**Design decisions for N6 (design thread, 2026-11-18; first proposals, the numbers live in `src/sim/rules/damage.ts` and are balanced in N7.5):**

- **The three shot.** Round shot is for the hull: a 32-pound ball goes through a brig's side out to about 400 m and through both sides inside 100 m; a 6-pound ball goes through bulwarks at any fighting range and through the side only inside about 150 m. Where a ball comes through, splinters fly inward in a cone and strike men within about 3 m; a ball that meets a gun dismounts it. Chain does not pierce the side: it cuts lines, tears sails and wounds spars about four times as well as round shot, out to 250 m. Grape pierces nothing thicker than a bulwark: it is for the men on deck and in the tops, and it riddles sails, inside 100 m.
- **How fast she can be lost.** Never to one broadside. A brig fought as hard as can be takes at least about four minutes of well-laid fire to be beaten, and most duels run ten to twenty. Five holes at the waterline left unplugged, with the pumps manned, sink her in about eight minutes; plugged and pumped she holds. A mast goes after three wounds in one of its parts, or when more than half the shrouds of one side are cut and the wind is on it. Fire reaches the magazine only if it is left unfought for three minutes or more.
- **Striking.** A human captain is never made to strike. When her state would make a computer captain strike, his first lieutenant says so ("We must strike, sir"), and he may fight on until she sinks under him. A computer captain strikes by that rule.
- **The captain** is never hit. Shot passing close staggers him, splinters fall round him, and the men beside him are not spared.
- **Violence** has three levels, the same simulation under each: mild (men fall and are carried below, nothing more is shown), plain (wounds and blood are seen), unsparing (a gun deck in action as it was). Plain is the default; Arnd sees all three at N6.6.

- **What the crew does unasked, and what an order adds** (confirmed 2026-11-18 as N6.3 proposed; all numbers first proposals). Unasked: the carpenter's crew of six pump while there is water in her; the carpenter and his mate plug holes below the water (about 45 s a hole) and, when nothing leaks, fish a wounded spar (one wound less); the boatswain and two mates knot and splice cut lines (about 60 s a line), the standing rigging that holds a spar first; the quartermasters rig the relieving tackles when the steering is shot away (four men, about 90 s); up to four men at hand beat at a fire beside them until a fire party comes. The carpenter's order only sets what his crew does first. The first lieutenant's orders take men from the guns, waisters first and then the last places of each gun's crew: "Hands to the pumps" is eight men (and frees the carpenter's crew to plug), "Fire party" eight men a fire, nearest the magazine first, "Clear the wreckage" six forecastle men with axes, about 60 s a fall.
- **The hurt.** The killed and the badly wounded leave the quarter bill at once. Each badly wounded man is carried below by the two nearest waisters, who then return; at most four such parties are away at one time, so a bloody minute does not empty the guns by itself. The lightly wounded fight on. In the simulation the killed stay where they fell; what is shown of them is the violence setting's business (at the mild level they too are carried below).

- **Beaten, burnt out, drawn, ended** (confirmed 2026-11-18 as N6.4 proposed). She is beaten (a computer captain strikes; a player's first lieutenant says "We must strike, sir") when any of these holds: half her men are killed or badly hurt; she cannot sail (no sail can draw) and fewer than four gun crews on both sides together can fire; the water is over two thirds of the level at which she founders and still gaining with the pumps manned. She is burnt out, lost like a ship sunk, when fire holds three or more spaces below at over half each or the light room is fully alight; the magazine still blows by its own rule if the fire reaches it first. A match is drawn when its time runs out with neither lost ("they part at nightfall"): a match setting, 40 minutes by default, and the master warns five minutes before. The end is one record (who lost, how: sunk, burnt, blown up, struck; when); both lost in the same step is a draw. The simulation goes on after the end so the ship is seen going down (her decks awash in about a minute), and every order after it is refused ("the fight is over"). Striking is asked twice: the first lieutenant answers "Strike, sir?", and given again within 10 s she strikes.

**Open design questions for N6:** none before G6 (sailing out of the fight is settled in [menu.md](menu.md)).

**Notes:** N6.1 (hits), `src/sim/damage.ts` with its geometry in `src/sim/damage/meet.ts` and its numbers in `src/sim/rules/damage.ts`.

- **Shots in flight.** step.ts puts every `fired` event of a step in flight with `launch`; `SimState.flying` keeps only which ship and gun fired each shot, in which tick, and each ball's spent punch (mm of oak, `GONE` once stopped or in the sea). The flight itself is kept beside the state, keyed by them (it is a pure function of the firing). Each step `stepHits` traces the piece of each ball's path flown in that step (from the step before this tick to this tick, so a shot fired in a step is first traced in the next) through every other ship as she lies at the step's start. Path time 0 is the firing tick: the gun captain's timing error (`late`) is in the aim, not in when the ball left. A machine that joins while a shot flies has no flight for it and drops it; the host's next state brings its damage.
- **Punch.** A ball goes through as much oak as `punchAt` gives at its speed (straight in v² between two speeds of the table, none below, never above `most`), spending it in order along its path: timber at its thickness over the cosine of its meeting (at most four times, `GLANCING`), a man `MAN_AS_OAK`, a spar its chord through it as fir, a gun `GUN_AS_OAK`. The flight is not slowed by what it went through: what it spent is kept instead. Timber it cannot pierce stops it (a lodged hole). Chain reaches lines, sails and spars `CHAIN_BITE` (4) times as far as a ball; a sail hit by chain counts four holes.
- **Her structure** is found by zones (outside, over her rail, the open deck, between decks, the hold), walked every 0.2 m along the piece and halved to the crossing: the side is the plan's planking cell by girth (`wale` in the wale's band), above the deck the bulwark cell, the decks' cells by level; a gun port's opening and a deck opening are no timber. Her rig is met as the rig plan lays it (yards square and hoisted, fore-and-aft sails amidships); a sail only while set, a square sail's clews lifted by its share. Braced and lowered yards and a sail's swing are not traced yet (the drawing will need the same rig pose, N6.5).
- **What is kept** (`SimShip.damage`, owner damage.ts, plain whole numbers): `holes` (one number a hole: its cell, its place in the cell to the damage map's 4 cm texel, the shot and gun, lodged or through; `holeAt` reads one; a panel with `STOVE_IN` holes is stove in, a bulwark section with `SMASHED` smashed out, `cellState`), `cut` (rig plan line places), `wounds` (one entry a wound, `sparParts` places: each spar's segment between two of its marks), `rent` (holes per sail, rig plan order: a count, not their places, to keep the state small; N6.5 places them seeded, and the `hit` events give the exact point while one is in view), `hurt` (man × 3 + wounded, badly wounded, killed; a man killed is met no more). A gun met is dismounted through guns.ts `dismountGun`. A hole takes about 5 bytes of the state.
- **Events:** `hit` (SimEvent) for each hole, lodged ball, splinters thrown, gun dismounted, man struck (his harm and whether by ball or splinter), spar wounded, line cut and sail holed, with where in her (along, across, height above her base). N6.5 draws them, N6.8 plays them; the order panel writes none of them. The captain is not in the simulation: N6.5 staggers him from the events and the shots' flights passing near him.
- **Cost** (cloud, software timing): a step with a round-shot broadside going through her about 2 to 4 ms at worst, a grape broadside about 6 to 9 ms in its worst step (nine balls a gun through her rig and crowd); the first ball near a ship also builds the crew's route network (about 0.3 s, once, warm it at load with the guns' laying tables). Steps with nothing near a ship cost nothing.
- **For N6.2:** flooding reads `holes` whose cells lie below her waterline as she lies (cell kind `planking`, height from the cell's girth band); a mast part with three `wounds` or a side's shrouds more than half `cut` with the wind on them falls (design decisions above). **For N6.3:** the crew still works with its hurt men: taking the `badly` and `killed` out of the quarter bill, and carrying the wounded below, is N6.3's. **For N3.12:** a spar fouled alongside is wounded through damage.ts (add a routine there beside `stepHits`, never a second writer). **For N7.5:** `PIERCE`, `SPLINTER_*` and `HURT` are first proposals; one broadside at 100 m in the trials here struck 15 to 20 of her 120 men.

**Notes:** N6.2 (what damage leads to), `stepAftermath` in `src/sim/damage.ts` (the one owner of all damage state, as for hits), with its rules in `src/sim/damage/flood.ts`, `fall.ts` and `fire.ts` and its numbers in `src/sim/rules/damage.ts`. It runs in each ship's part of the step, after her crew and orders and before she sails.

- **Water.** Each planking hole not plugged by its lodged ball lets the sea in by `inflow`: an opening `RAGGED` (1.7) times the ball across, `DISCHARGE` 0.6, under the head from the sea's surface at the hole (the real sea, as she lies) less the water inside standing over it. Kept as tonnes in her larboard and starboard halves (`flooded`): the sea comes into its side's half and the halves run toward the level for her heel over `CROSS_SECONDS` (12 s, through her stowage), so the holes' side lists her a little and water left in her runs to the low side as she rolls. The pumps (two, 4 men each at 1.5 t a minute) are worked by the men the quarter bill posts at them who are there and fit (6 men, 2.25 t a minute). The water's weight goes to floating as a `Burden` (motion.ts: weight, turning moment and inertia where it lies) and to sailing (heavier to move). Held to the design: five carronade holes 0.3 m under her waterline (what her roll and the sea make of a hole at it) founder her in about 8 minutes with the pumps manned; a 6-pound hole is held by the pumps, a 32-pound one a metre down is not. `founderTonnes` (about 300 t: her lowest deck edge at the calm sea) is for N6.4.
- **Spars falling.** `fallsDue`: a spar part with three wounds breaks there; more than half of a spar's weather shrouds and backstays gone (cut, or fast to a spar that is down) with the apparent wind across her at 3 m/s or more, or half its stays gone with its mast's square sails aback, brings it down at the part above where it is held (a lower mast at her partners, a topmast at the cap). It goes over the lee side with every spar stepped on it, its sails and the lines fast to it (`standingOf`); the men aloft on it are thrown down (`HURT.fall`); it lies `alongside` dragging her by `WRECK_DRAG` (a lower mast all but stops her and swings her toward it) until cut away. - **Fire.** A ball through her timber or into a gun sets her afire by `FIRE_CHANCE` (1 in 20 fired from within 40 m, the wads; 3 in 1,000 farther), in the plan's space where it struck. A fire's size is the share of its space alight; unfought it doubles every 78 s, at half its space it catches the spaces the plan's ways join it to, and the powder goes up when the magazine's own fire reaches a quarter (from a fire started there, about 3 minutes: the design's least). Until fire parties come (N6.3) the fit men in the space and the spaces joined to it fight it (at most 8, each putting out 0.12 % of the space a second): a fire on the open deck dies at once, one below with no one near grows. `blownUp` and the `blew up` event are for N6.4. Growth is a fixed factor a step (+ and × only).
- **Steering.** A ball within 0.4 m of the line from the wheel to her rudder head shoots it away (`steering` false, `hit` event `steering`): her rudder trails amidships whatever the helmsman does, until N6.3 mends it.
- **Events:** `hit` gains `steering` and `fire` (a ball's doing); `aftermath` (SimEvent) says a spar `fell` (its part and side), a `man` was thrown down, fire spread to a space (`fire`) or went `out`, and the magazine `blew up`. The order panel writes none; the cries come with the phrase book (N4.5).

**Notes:** N6.3 (repairs and the wounded), the repairs wanted each step worked out by `repairsDue` in `src/sim/crew/repairs.ts` from her damage, what she is told and where her men are; the crew (crew.ts, still the one owner of every man's task and place) makes a task of each and gives back those finished, and damage.ts `mend` does them to her (the one routine that mends). - **Repairs are tasks.** A repair's task has no act (`NO_ACT`) and a spot of its own (`at`: the hole, the fire, the wounded man, the surgeon) where all its men go; it is manned by a count of men, not by skill, and kept while it is still wanted (a party is not sent from one hole to the next). Pumping and fighting a fire are standing jobs: dropped when the water is out or the fire is out, never finished. Who mans them: ranks (the carpenter's crew, the boatswain and his mates, the quartermasters), parts (forecastle men, waisters), the nearest first; or the first lieutenant's quarter bill (`billOrder`: the waisters first, then the last places of each gun, the gun captains last), wherever the men are. The spot of a repair is the nearest a man can stand on within 3 m of where the work is, worked out once per hole, space, spar part and line.
- **Unasked:** the carpenter and his mate plug the holes in her planking up to 0.6 m above her waterline (`PLUG_ABOVE`), the deepest first, the rest of his crew pumping at their post; with nothing leaking they fish the spar part with the most wounds. The boatswain and his mates splice the cut lines that would hold something again (not those fast to a spar that is down), standing rigging that holds a spar first. The quartermasters rig the relieving tackles at the tiller (`TILLER_SPACE`, the breadroom). A full run from the deck: the plug begins after about 13 s and is done at about 58 s, the splice at about 67 s, the tackles at about 96 s.

**Design changes that go with it** (the first duels showed where the rules, not only the numbers, were wrong):

- **The start is by time, not by distance.** The ships start as far apart as they close in about three minutes under battle sail in that wind (about 700 m in force 2 to 3, up to 1,500 m in force 5 to 6); N7.5a made it three and a half (`START_MINUTES`), since they open fire about half a minute before they would meet, so the first broadside comes about three minutes in. This replaces the fixed 1,500 m of the N3 decisions.
- **"Hull" means between wind and water.** Gun captains ordered to fire at her hull lay their guns at her waterline, not at her side 1.25 m up, as gunners did: with her roll and the waves a fair share of those balls strike below the water, and that is what sinks ships. A plugged hole still weeps, so water gains slowly even with a good carpenter.
- **A helpless ship is beaten.** A ship that cannot sail, facing one that still can and still has half her guns, is beaten: the other lies off her bow or stern and rakes her. This joins the three conditions of N6.4.
- **Splinters and grape hurt.** About half the men struck are killed or badly hurt, not a quarter, so a well-served broadside thins a gun deck and the fire slackens on the losing side: the fight tips instead of grinding on.
- **Flooding is a contest, not a switch.** The first duels had ships either dry (under 7 t) or lost (200 t), with nothing between. Wanted: the carpenter's crew at the pumps holds about two open holes at the waterline; "Hands to the pumps" doubles that; each plug takes its time and more holes arrive while he works, so water rises and falls with the fight and the captain sees it coming, is told by the carpenter, and can answer by sending men or by turning her wounded side away from the enemy so the holes lift clear.
- **Beaten, first pass** (N7.5a, confirmed 2026-11-18): three men in ten killed or badly hurt (first a quarter, raised 2026-11-18 because grape at 100 m ended 4 fights in 100 inside four minutes), not half (only about 70 stand exposed on deck, so half was never reached); by water at 0.95 of the level at which she founders, not two thirds, so a ship strikes for water only when she is truly going. These replace the two numbers in the N6 decisions. Fire is a contest too: it outgrows the two men at hand, so a fire party must come within about a minute.
- **Skill must pay through decisions, not through a better crew** (2026-11-18; N7.5a found master beating green only about 1 in 2 with equal crews, the luck of the waterline holes deciding). The game's promise is that the better captain wins, and between two players the crews are always equal, so the rules must make a captain's choices decide the fight: (1) less luck in flooding: water comes from many small contributions that add up with the fire she takes, not from one or two lucky holes; (2) the held broadside pays: guns fired together on the captain's word at the right moment of the roll strike where they are aimed clearly more often than guns fired as they bear, and at the wrong moment clearly less; (3) the weather position pays: the smoke of both ships drifts down on the leeward ship and spoils her gun captains' aim while it hangs, and the windward ship chooses the range; (4) position pays: lying on her bow or quarter where few of her guns bear, and above all raking her. A computer captain's level may also stand for his crew's drill, but only a little (gun drill about 5 % slower for green and 5 % quicker for master), as flavour for playing alone, never as the thing that makes him win. A player's crew is drilled as seasoned, the same for every player. The row "one level up wins about 3 in 4" stays as the measure of all this and is not part of the failing check until it is met.
- **Raking pays.** A ball that enters by the bow or stern travels the length of the deck, and each such ball counts for several fired into her side. The computer captain already tries for it; the damage must reward it.

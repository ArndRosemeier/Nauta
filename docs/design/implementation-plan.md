# Nauta: implementation plan

What is built, in which order, and when each piece is done. The design is in [nauta-plan.md](nauta-plan.md); this file turns its build order (N0 to N7) into milestones and work packages that a coding thread can pick up one at a time.

Written 2026-11-17 by the design thread. Arnd decides at the gates; design questions go to the design thread.

## How work is done

**Roles.** The design thread owns this plan and the design. Coding threads build work packages. Arnd judges each milestone at its gate, on the iPad.

**A coding thread takes one work package** (`N1.3`), named in its first message. Before writing code it reads [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), [nauta-plan.md](nauta-plan.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), [SEAMS.md](../SEAMS.md) and the package below with everything it depends on.

**Each package lands like this:**

1. Work on a branch. Keep `npm test` and `npm run build` green.
2. Add the package's tests, its rows in SEAMS.md, its owners in `tools/guards/rules.json`, its numbers as named constants or rule tables, and its what's-new note `whatsnew/N1.3-<what>.txt` ([whatsnew/README.md](../../whatsnew/README.md)): what Arnd can now see or do on the iPad, in plain words, with no version number.
3. Take the package's screenshots or run its check, and look at them.
4. Open a pull request with `PLAN: N1.3` and a `COPIES:` line, and squash-merge it into `main` once CI is green (one commit is one build, one version). No review by Arnd is needed for a package. `main` deploys to https://arndrosemeier.github.io/Nauta/.
5. In the same pull request, set the package's status in this file to `done` and note anything the next package must know under "Notes" of its milestone.

**Stop and ask the design thread** (through Arnd) when a package needs a decision this plan does not make, when "done when" cannot be met as written, or when the work wants to change a fixed rule, a budget or another package's seam. Do not decide design in a coding thread.

**Status values:** `open`, `in work` (with the thread's name), `done`, `blocked` (with the reason).

**A milestone is finished at its gate:** Arnd opens the deployed page on the iPad Air M4 and judges the gate's list. Until he says yes, the milestone stays open and the next one's packages that depend on it wait.

## Budgets

These hold from N1 on. They are proposals until Arnd confirms them at gate G1; after that, changing one needs his OK.

| Budget | Value on the reference tablet (iPad Air M4, Safari) |
| --- | --- |
| Frame rate | 60 frames a second, steady: 95 of 100 frames within 16.7 ms, none over 33 ms in a 5 minute fight |
| Sea, all of it | at most 4 ms of a frame |
| Crew, all of it (animation and drawing, 2 ships) | at most 4 ms of a frame |
| Both ships drawn (hull, fittings, rigging, sails, damage, her shadow map) | at most 3 ms of a frame; at most 250 draw calls and 1.5 million triangles |
| Powder smoke at its worst | at most 2 ms of a frame |
| Sky, light, the grade and the interface | at most 2 ms of a frame |
| Ship memory (geometry, textures, damage maps) | at most 250 MB of the page's 1.2 GB |
| Ship download (models and textures, both navies) | at most 20 MB of the duel's 60 MB |
| Building her at load, in a worker | at most 3 s of the 15 s from tap to deck |
| Shader compile on the render path | none (docs/SHADERS.md) |
| Crew memory (both crews: bodies, dress, motions) | at most 150 MB of the page's 1.2 GB |
| Page memory | at most 1.2 GB |
| First load (download before the title shows) | at most 15 MB; everything for a duel at most 60 MB |
| Time from tap to standing on deck | at most 15 s on a good connection |
| Simulation step | fixed, 30 a second; one step of a full duel at most 2 ms on the tablet |
| Network | host sends at most 20 states a second, each at most 16 KB |

**The frame's shares fit together at the worst moment** (design thread, 2026-11-17, after the ship design's warning that sea 5, crew 5, ship 3 and smoke 3 ms left nothing for the rest). A broadside in smoke with both crews in sight is sea 4 + crew 4 + ships 3 + smoke 2 + sky, light and interface 2 = 15 ms, which leaves 1.7 ms spare in the 16.7 ms frame. How each share is kept: the sea's compute costs next to nothing on the iPad (the probe), so its 4 ms is shading, and the part of the sea hidden behind the ship and the smoke is not shaded; men below deck are drawn only through openings (the ship's rooms and openings, N2.4), and distant and hidden men animate at a lower rate (N4.0 fixes the rates); smoke is drawn at half resolution and joined to the frame by depth; and one render scale, a quality setting, is the first knob when a frame runs long. No share may borrow from another: a package that cannot keep its share comes to the design thread.

`?bench` (package N1.9) measures the frame and memory budgets on the device itself and shows the result on screen, since the tablet has no developer tools at hand.

## Cross-cutting rules for every package

- **The simulation is the game.** Everything that decides anything lives in `src/sim`, pure and seeded, stepped at a fixed rate. The renderer and the sound only show state and events.
- **One state, serialisable.** The whole match is one plain data value (`SimState`) that can be sent, saved and compared. No simulation state hides in a closure or a class instance elsewhere.
- **Events for what happens in an instant** (a gun fires, a spar parts, a man falls): the simulation emits them, drawing and sound consume them. A package that adds something audible or visible adds its event, never a callback into the renderer.
- **Rules as data** in `src/sim/rules/` (one file per subject). A package that needs a tuning number adds it there, with unit and source.
- **Quality levels** come from one place (`src/app/quality.ts`, N1.8). A package that costs frame time adds its knob there; it never checks for "tablet" itself.
- **Sound with the feature.** Each package lists its sounds; they are made through `@futuremagic/audio`'s tool and OpenRouter into `sounds.json`, several seeded takes each. No voices.
- **Touch first.** Every interaction a package adds works with a finger before it gets a key.
- **Shared code moves, it is not copied.** Code that MasterWorld already has and Nauta needs is moved into a shared package by a pull request in AssetGenerator, then both games use it.

## N1 The sea

The new ocean: better than MasterWorld's from deck height, in calm, breeze and gale, within its 4 ms on the tablet. Everything later floats on it, so it is built with the most care.

**The design in one paragraph.** The sea is one function of seed, wind and time. Its long waves (those a hull feels) are a seeded set of wave components computed in `src/sim` on the CPU, the same on every machine. The renderer displaces the surface by exactly these components, so a hull sits in the water it is drawn in. Shorter waves, ripples, foam and light are added by the renderer on top and never affect the simulation.

For N1.4: the sky's environment map is `sky.envTexture` (the env copy is drawn at `ENV_SATURATION` of its colour; a mirror puts it back with `fullSky`), the sun's light as it falls is `sky.sunColor` and `sky.lightDirection`, the day's light `sky.light` (`SkyLight`); the scene's lights carry `sky.gain`. **Notes:** N1.6 (wind) is in `src/sim/wind.ts`, its tuning in `src/sim/rules/wind.ts` (forces 0 to 8) and the Beaufort scale in `src/sim/rules/beaufort.ts` (one table for wind and sea). A match's wind is `makeWind(seed, force, fromBearing)`, plain data. `meanWind(state, t)` is the wind without gusts (the sea's driver); `windAt(state, x, z, t)` adds the gusts (the sails'). Both give `{ speed, toward, x, z, from, gust }`: `toward` is the sea's angle (0 = +x, a quarter turn = +z), `from` a compass bearing (0 north = -z, clockwise, `units.ts#bearingDir`). The mean veers slowly, each swing taking 10 to 30 minutes, within the preset's limit (45° in calm down to 10° from force 6), and freshens a little; whether the sea follows the veer or keeps its start value is the sea's choice. For N1.4: `gustsNear(state, x, z, t, radius)` lists the patches (heart, radius, strength now, peak), and the shader draws each with `gustShape` (one at the heart, (1 - d²)² to the rim); patches drift with the mean wind. Gust, veer and freshen numbers are first proposals, to be tuned by eye at N1.4 and by feel at N3.3.

N1.1 (sea state) is in `src/sim/sea/`, its tuning in `src/sim/rules/sea.ts`. A sea is `startSea(seed, wind, t)`, plain data; call `followWind(sea, meanWind(windState, t), t)` every step and the sea takes a new wave set only when the mean wind has moved by 1.5 m/s or 15°, fading it in over 3 minutes (`changeWind` forces one). Each wave set is 25 Gerstner waves (`SEA_COMPONENTS`; with the swell's 3 the sea sums 28, and 32 missed the 1 ms on CI) from a JONSWAP spectrum (0.6 to 2.5 times the peak frequency) with a cos-2s spread, equal energy per wave, so the set's significant height is the preset's. For N1.3: `seaAt(sea, x, z, t)` is a calm point and where its water is now, exactly what the vertex stage must do: for each layer, factor `f` from `layerWeights(sea, t)`, and for each wave θ = kx·x + kz·z − omega·t + phase, height += f·amp·cos θ, shift −= f·reach·(kx, kz)·sin θ, plus f²·lift per layer (the lift keeps the mean level at zero under Gerstner's wide troughs). The renderer's short waves start below the set's `shortest` wavelength. For N2.2: `surfaceAt(sea, x, z, t)` is the water under a world point (Newton on the shift, about 1.2 µs each against 0.5 µs for `seaAt`), with normal and water velocity; `stretch` is below 1 on crests and never reaches 0 (the simulated sea does not fold; foam from folding is the short waves'). Sines and cosines in the hot loop are `trig.ts#sincos`, built from + and × only and without a branch, so they round alike on every engine and cost a third of Math.sin plus Math.cos; the wave set itself uses Math.exp and Math.cos once per set, which engines may round differently in the last bit, so machines should compare the host's state, not sea samples. Answered by the design thread below (N1.10 adds a swell; heights are judged at N1.4).

N1.10 (the swell) is in `src/sim/sea/swell.ts`, its tuning (`SWELL_*`) in `src/sim/rules/sea.ts`. Every sea carries `seaSwell`, one more wave set beside its layers: 3 waves of 9 to 12 s, 0.6 m significant height together, drawn once by `startSea` from the sea's seed and the starting wind's direction (every wave at least 40° off it, within 8° of the swell's own direction), never changed by `changeWind` or `followWind`, the same at every force. For N1.3: `seaAt` adds the swell first at factor 1 (no fade), with its own `lift` (f² = 1), then the layers as before; so the vertex stage sums `seaSwell` plus each layer by `layerWeights`. The swell's waves are 125 to 225 m long, so on a small patch of sea it shows as the whole surface rising and falling slowly. The wind set's `shortest` still marks where the renderer's short waves begin. The wind sets' fold limit stays 0.7; the swell takes what two crossfading sets leave below 1 (`SWELL_FOLD_LIMIT`, about 0.01), which holds its choppiness near 0.7 to 0.8. A preset's height becomes √(preset² + 0.6²): a calm 0.6 m, force 3 0.85 m, force 6 3.06 m.

N1.3 (the sea's surface mesh) is in `src/render/`: `seaGrid.ts` lays the sea out as a quadtree of square tiles around the eye (CDLOD's layout), every tile one shared grid of 16 × 16 cells, level k being 4 m × 2^k wide on a fixed lattice of its own size (so vertices never swim), 0.25 m cells under the eye, drawn out to 1.25 times the horizon's distance plus 500 m. A level reaches 5 tile sides from the eye; in its last fifth its vertices slide onto the next coarser grid (`seaMorph`, mirrored in the vertex stage), so levels meet without cracks and tiles split or join without a pop (test/seaGrid.test.ts checks both). `seaWaves.ts` hands the vertex stage `seaAt`'s waves as one weighted list with each phase worked out in double precision near the eye (`forEachShownSet` in `sea.ts` is the one place that lists the sets showing), so the drawn surface is `seaAt`'s to a millimetre 60 km out and hours into a match. A wave is drawn fully where it spans 6 cells of the grid and fades out by 3 (`SEA_WAVE_FULL_CELLS`, `SEA_WAVE_GONE_CELLS`): far waves fade into a flat sea instead of flickering, and near the eye waves shorter than about 1.5 m are left to the fine sea (N1.2), which at forces 1 and 2 includes the simulated set's shortest waves. The earth's curve (`earth.ts#earthDrop`) bends the drawn sea down around the eye, so the horizon is a true one (3.57 √h km off) and a clean line. For N1.4: `seaMaterial.ts` has a plain stand-in look (sky colour by Fresnel, a sun glint, a dark body, haze toward the horizon's colour, all from `SkyView.light`) to replace; the vertex stage passes the surface normal (`vSeaNormal`) and the vector to the eye (`vSeaToEye`), and the glint shows the grid's facets where it is sharp, so N1.4's normals should come per pixel. For N1.8: the knobs are `SEA_TILE_CELLS`, `SEA_FINEST_CELL` and `SEA_LOD_RANGE` in `seaGrid.ts`; at the defaults the camera sees 200 to 330 tiles of 289 vertices, each summing the 28 waves (53 during a change of wind). For N2.5 and N3.8: everything drawn far off must sink by `earthDrop` like the sea, or it floats; the sea's mesh has its own origin, stepping by 1024 m with the eye. `?eye=` (metres, 0.5 to 200) and `?wind=` (Beaufort force 0 to 8) now set the scene (`src/app/params.ts`); N1.9 adds to them. Checked headless with WebGL 2 (`?gl`) at 3 m, 10 m and 30 m, looking level, down and across, in a breeze and a gale; on WebGPU the sea's shader was compiled headless without a message, but no frame was drawn: the cloud's software WebGPU loses its device before the first frame, on `main` before this package too, so WebGPU was checked on Arnd's PC instead (Chrome, RTX 5070): 16.7 ms a frame (vsync) at 3, 4.5 and 30 m and in a gale, the shader built in the warm-up with no compile later, no holes below the horizon, and the horizon a clean line within one pixel row; the iPad look is still to come.

N1.4 (the sea's material) is `seaMaterial.ts`, with its parts beside it. Per pixel the normal sums every simulated wave and the fine waves (`seaFine.ts`: 28 seeded waves per wind layer from just below the simulated set's shortest down to 0.5 m, faded in and out with their layer), each faded out by the pixel's footprint (`PIXEL_FULL`, `PIXEL_GONE`), and below 0.5 m the ripples (`seaRipples.ts`: a tiling texture of 96 ripples down to 3.5 cm, laid twice at two sizes turned a quarter to each other, drifting their own ways; single waves that short drew a regular weave under the eye). How rough the sea is comes from Cox and Munk's mean square slope for the wind (glassy below 0.6 m/s); what the simulated waves carry is taken off, the fine waves and ripples carry the rest, and whatever a pixel cannot draw becomes roughness (LEAN-like), so the sky's reflection blurs and the sun's and moon's glitter (Beckmann facets) widens with distance and wind instead of flickering. The sky is the rig's environment map (`fullSky`, Karis's fit for its strength); the water's body is a deep blue lit by the sun and the sky, greener where foam has filled it and greyer when stirred in a gale; light shows through the thin tops of crests toward the sun; haze toward the horizon's colour. Foam forms where the waves bunch (the vertex stage's stretch, now and 1.6 s and 4 s ago at the same water, so it lingers and fades); the level that turns white is set each moment so the share of white water is Monahan's whitecap share for the wind (`seaFoam.ts#foamLevels`), broken up by a seeded pattern (fresh foam with bubbles, old foam thinning to winding strands). Gusts are a 128² map around the eye (`seaGusts.ts`) that roughens the fine waves (a light air's gust shows dark on glassy water). From below the sea shows Snell's window, total reflection outside it, and murk with distance (the 'deep water' kind, a sphere around the eye when it is under the surface). On WebGPU, building the sea's shader in the warm-up left the sky's environment map blank until the rig drew it again, which with the clock stopped never came: `SkyRig.redrawEnvironment()` (AssetGenerator) is called once after the warm-up. `?yaw=` and `?pitch=` (degrees) aim the view and `?eye=` goes down to −40 m for views from under water. Checked on Arnd's PC (WebGPU, RTX 5070): 16.7 ms a frame (vsync) at dawn, noon, dusk and night, calm, breeze and gale, and under water, ten shaders in the warm-up and none later, no console errors; the iPad's cost is still to measure. For N1.8: the knobs are `SEA_FINE_PER_LAYER` (each fine wave is a sine per pixel near the eye), `PIXEL_FULL`/`PIXEL_GONE` and the ripples' two texture reads. For N2.5: foam and wake enter through the material's foam (`vSeaFoam`: fresh and old foam per vertex) and the body's aerated colour; far off, foam on the coarse tiles is still a soft-edged sheet. For N1.9: wave heights, swell and gusts were judged by eye here and left at their values; `?day=0` stops the clock but not the sea, so standard shots need a fixed sea time.

N1.7 (sound of sea and wind) is in `src/audio/`: `ambience.ts` decides the beds, `slaps.ts` the sounds placed near the listener, `tuning.ts` holds every number, and `seaSound.ts` plays them through `@futuremagic/audio`'s `AudioEngine` (main.ts calls `sound.hear(...)` each frame with the camera's ear, facing and crown, and `ship.below`). The sea's beds (calm, breeze, strong breeze, gale) crossfade at equal power by the force of the sea (`seaForce`: each wave layer's wind as a fractional Beaufort force, `beaufortForce`, weighted by its energy share), so the sound changes over the same 3 minutes as the waves and never ahead of them; the wind's beds (light, fresh, strong, gale) by `windAt`'s speed at the ear, gusts included, gliding in 0.6 s; a deep swell surge rises with the water's vertical speed under the ear. Every bed is two 30 s takes blended by a slow seeded swing (47 to 113 s, each bed its own), on top of the engine's two chains at rates 1 and 0.97, so the mix does not come round within minutes. Eight points on the water 3 to 11 m around the ear are watched; when a crest passes one (the water there stops rising, above 0.6 of the sea's RMS height) a seeded share starts a sound there: laps in a calm, hull slaps from force 2, crests breaking into foam from force 3, heavy seas bursting from force 5.5, about one a second in a breeze and never closer than 0.3 s. Under water (`?eye=-3`) the world is heard through a 350 Hz low-pass, below deck through 900 Hz until N2.8 makes below deck sound right. Sound starts on the first tap: the engine (AssetGenerator #185) now listens to every press for the page's life, since Safari accepts only a tap's end or a click and suspends the context in the background, and plays a silent sample in the press; Safari's audio session is set to `playback`, so the sound is heard with the device set to silent. The takes are in `public/sound/` (4.5 MB in all, 64 kbit/s mono at 32 kHz, fetched after the first tap and only what the weather asks for, about 2 MB at one force); their prompts, how they were made and checked are in `tools/sound/` (README and sounds.json; raw takes in the project's shared folder). Decoded, a bed is 3.8 MB and nine play between two forces, so the engine keeps up to 48 MB (`SOUND_MEMORY`). Checked headless: the first click and the first tap open the sound, the beds load and play at the levels above, slaps start. Not checked: how it sounds. No listening model is reliable (one heard "surf on a beach" in most sea takes, the first round's were); Arnd judges by ear at G1, and any take is replaced with `make.mjs --again <id>`. For N2.8: the ship's own sounds (timber, water along the hull, below deck) go beside these, through the same engine (`SeaSound.engine`); the muffle below deck is a stand-in to replace. For N3.9: wind in the rigging follows the apparent wind; these wind beds are the true wind on the ear and should duck under it. N1.8 (quality levels) is `src/app/quality.ts`: `QUALITY_LEVELS` (high, medium, low, lowest) is the one table of knobs, and `QualityChooser` picks a level from measured frame time. Every device starts at high and steps down while too many frames are late, so nothing tells tablet from desktop by the browser's name (Safari on the iPad says Mac). After the first second it measures a level for 1.5 s of frames (after 0.5 s to settle); a frame later than 25 ms (one and a half frames at 60 fps, so a display's vsync showed the frame before twice) is late, and a level holds while at most 5 % of its frames are late. It never steps up: with vsync the frame time does not show how much room is left, and a GPU timer would need the Stage's renderer to ask for `timestamp-query` (AssetGenerator). A hidden page drops what it had measured of the level (`interrupt`). The level and why show on the HUD's second line (`?hud`), with the pixels drawn; `?quality=high|medium|low|lowest` fixes a level and measures nothing (for shots and the bench). The knobs are only those that change without a compile, as the level changes while the game runs: `renderScale` (the share of the device's pixels along each side, on top of the Stage's cap of 2 per CSS pixel: 1, 0.8, 0.65, 0.5, so lowest draws the iPad's screen at one pixel per CSS pixel), `seaCells` (16 or 8 cells a sea tile: the tile keeps the densest grid's vertices and the index holds a run of triangles for every density, chosen by the draw range, and the shader's `spacing` uniform makes the sparse grid's odd vertices slide onto the coarser grid as the dense grid's do, so levels still meet without cracks) and, for N1.2, `fineSeaBands` and `fineSeaSize` (read by nothing yet). N1.2 (the fine sea on the GPU) is `seaFine.ts` (pure: the spectrum laid out in bands) and `seaBands.ts` (the GPU), replacing N1.4's 28 per-pixel fine waves per layer. The short waves from 48 m down to 0.5 m (the ripples' texture still takes over below) are one spectrum, the tail of the simulated set's (height density ∝ k⁻³, an equal slope per octave), its level set so that they carry Cox and Munk's slope less what the simulated waves carry, exactly the budget N1.4's waves had; they begin at 0.9 of the set's shortest wave and fade in over a third of an octave, with a cos-2s spread from s = 2 at 48 m to 0.5 at 0.5 m. That range is split into bands equal on a log scale: at `high` three of 256², 48 to 10.5 m, 10.5 to 2.3 m and 2.3 to 0.5 m, on tiles of 384, 79 and 16 m (a tile holds 8 of its longest waves, or fewer where its shortest would span under 4 texels, and the tiles are nudged 6 % apart so no two share a period). Each frame one compute pass builds every row's spectrum of the moment (the two strongest wind layers, weighted in energy, so a change of wind neither jumps nor swells) and transforms it, then one pass per band transforms its columns (Stockham's radix-2 FFT in workgroup memory, one workgroup per line: four dispatches in all, where a pass per stage would take 16 per band) and writes two rgba16float textures per band: `shape` (the water's sideways shift and height) and `slope` (slope along x and z, the bunching (the shift's divergence), and the foam a fold leaves, lingering e^(−t / 4 s)). Frequencies sit on whole multiples of 2π / 4096 s, so the GPU's clock wraps without losing precision. Only `slope` has mipmaps (three makes them each frame, one small render pass per level); a stage may write only four storage textures, hence a column pass per band. In the material every band's slope adds to the normal (gusts grow the short bands more), the part of a band too fine for the pixel is roughness (its range's share, on a log scale, below the pixel's `PIXEL_GONE` length), and the vertex stage adds the bands' shape where the grid holds them (fully where a band's shortest wave spans 3 cells, gone where its middle one no longer does). Foam where waves fold: fresh foam is now made per pixel from the simulated waves' stretch (`vSeaFoam.x` now carries the raw stretch) plus the bands' bunching, white where it lies `foamDepth` deviations below the mean of what the pixel draws, so the white share stays Monahan's at every distance and a crest breaks in ragged patches where the short waves crowd on it; where the bands are too fine for the pixel the broad noise breaks it up as before. Old foam is still the vertex stage's lagged stretch, its lace now partly the bands' fold foam. The quality knobs (N1.8's `fineSeaBands`, `fineSeaSize`) change nothing compiled: every size the levels name gets a band set at the start (`high` to `low` share the 256² set, `lowest` has 128²), all compiled in the warm-up (`FineSea.warmUp`, `compileComputeAsync`); the material reads as many bands as the most any level asks for, switched by a uniform. WebGPU is required: `createRenderer` asks for `webgpu` only, a browser without it gets one plain sentence, and `?gl` is gone. Headless: Chromium's software GPU renders WebGPU but cannot present it to a canvas (the device lost before the first frame in N1.3's note was this), so `npm run shot` injects a stand-in swap chain (`tools/shot/swapchain.js`) and paints the last frame for the picture; CI's browser check runs on WebGPU now, and shot also prints three's warnings (a shader node that fails to build is only a warning, and its mesh is not drawn). Checked in the cloud on that software GPU: the band textures read back carry each band's slope to 5 %; shots in calm, breeze and gale, at 3, 4.5 and 30 m and at `lowest`, no errors, 15 shaders in the warm-up and none later. Not verified on a real GPU tonight: the iPad's cost (the estimate is four compute dispatches over 3 × 256² and 24 tiny mipmap passes a frame; the probe's FFT-shaped chain took 0.125 ms there), its look on the iPad, and whether a band's tile shows from deck height on a real display. Reading the 1 ms off the iPad: `?bench` (N1.9) times whole frames, not passes, so it shows the bands' cost only as part of the frame; if the frame holds its refresh at `high` with room to spare, the bands fit, and their own time needs the per-pass GPU timing N1.9 left for later (three's `trackTimestamp` in `@futuremagic/stage`). The bands run in `seaView.fine.run(renderer)` before the frame is drawn; their mipmaps are made while the sea is drawn. The headless shots also showed N2.4's damage map uploaded empty (its byte count read before it was set, so the bundle made a map of no bytes, a WebGPU validation error on every page); fixed here in `shipMaterials.ts`. For N2.5: wake foam can join the per-pixel `fresh` in the material; the bands' shape is not in `surfaceAt` (a few centimetres at most).

**Design decisions for N1 (design thread, 2026-11-17; Arnd may overrule at G1):** a flat calm keeps a swell from elsewhere (N1.10), because a mirror-still sea looks dead and a ship should always breathe under your feet. A duel is fought in force 2 to 6: below that the ships do not move, above it the guns cannot be worked; force 0 and the gale exist for looking at the sea. Wave heights and periods stay at the open-sea values until they are judged by eye at N1.4, then the rule table is tuned once, there.

**Open design questions for N1 (design thread):** the Beaufort presets a duel may have (calm to near gale: a full gale makes gunnery impossible and may be for later); whether weather changes during a match.

**Design decisions for N3 (design thread, 2026-11-18; Arnd judges the feel at G3 and may overrule):**

- **Pace.** N3.3's first proposal stands as built: the speed table (plain sail, force 5: no way within 60° of the wind, 5 knots close-hauled, 10 on the beam, 11 to 11.5 on the quarter, 10 running), turns about three times as tight as a real brig's, a tack at speed through the wind in 20 to 55 s, stays missed from about 1 knot with about two and a half minutes to gather way again. Nothing here is tuned further before Arnd has sailed her at G3; what he judges there is whether closing takes too long, whether a tack feels like a risk worth taking, and whether a missed tack punishes too hard.
- **Heaving to stops her.** "Heave to" is one order that does all of it: the main yards laid aback, the courses hauled up, the boom sail brailed, the helm put down. She loses her way within about a minute in a fresh breeze (force 5 and up) and lies still enough to fight or wait; in light winds the heavy hull coasts longer (about 100 s in force 4, 150 s in force 3, over four minutes in force 2, as N3.4 measured). That is left as it is for Arnd to feel at G3. Backing a mast alone stays possible as its own order.
- **A course she cannot lie is refused in words, not obeyed into irons.** Ordered to steer closer than close-hauled, the master answers that she will not lie that course (an event with the reason) and steers close-hauled on the nearer tack. Tacking is the order that takes her through the wind: a course order never does. A course on the other tack is reached by wearing, and a course she cannot lie that falls on the other tack ends close-hauled on that tack, by wearing; a course in the wind's eye itself keeps the tack she is on. Whenever a course order makes her wear, the master says so (an event, "Wearing round, sir"), so the captain is never surprised by the long way round and can order a tack instead.
- **The start.** The two ships start 1,500 m apart with the wind on the beam of the line between them, each under battle sail and steering to close, so neither has the weather position and the first decision is who works for it. The match's seed turns the whole picture round the compass and picks the force (2 to 6). Other starts (a chase, one ship to windward) come with the match settings in N7.

- **A sail's state is one number the crew moves** (2026-11-18, found by N3.10). How far each sail is set is a share from furled to sheeted home, held in the ship's state and advanced by the crew's work on it; the wind's force on the sail grows with it, and the drawing shows exactly that share, with no timer of its own. So what the captain sees being set is what is already driving her, on every machine.
- **Every sail she carries draws on every course the master accepts** (2026-11-18, found by N3.10: full and by left square sails aback and shivering). "Full and by" means as close as she lies with every set sail full: the helmsman steers by the sails, not by a fixed angle. A sail shivers or lies aback only while she turns through the wind, when a mast is backed by order, or when she is caught by a shift, and then the master says so. `check:sailing` holds this for every wind and canvas.

**Notes:** N3.1 (simulation frame), `src/sim/state.ts`, `step.ts`, `codec.ts`, `replay.ts`.
- **The state** is `SimState { matchSeed, tick, wind, sea, ships }`, plain data with no class, closure or typed array in it. The clock is `tick`, a whole count of steps; `simTime(state)` is `tick / 30`, worked out, never summed, so every machine has the same time to the bit. `startMatch({ seed, windForce, windFrom, ships: [{ x, z, heading }] })` makes the wind and the sea from seeds derived from the match's seed (`'wind'`, `'sea'`) and puts each ship still on her line. A ship is `{ motion, course }`, known by her place in `ships`; how the two start (distance, who has the wind) is still the caller's, an open question above.
- **A step** (`step(state, orders) -> { state, events }`): the orders in the order given (the host's list is the truth: two orders for one ship in a step, the later stands), then the sea follows the wind's mean at the step's start (`followWind`), then each ship floats one `stepMotion` on that sea under her `course`. It never changes the state it is given.
- **Orders for now:** one kind, `{ kind: 'course', ship, heading, speed }`, taken at once (the heading wrapped, the speed 0 to `TOP_SPEED`, 12 knots, in rules/ships.ts). An order that cannot be taken gives the event `{ kind: 'refused', tick, order, reason }`. `course` is owned by step.ts as a stand-in: **for N3.3**, the sailing model takes over heading and speed (rules.json's `course` row moves to sailing.ts or goes), and its forces (sail heel, damping) go through motion.ts, which owns the pose; **for N3.4**, the vocabulary moves to orders.ts and replaces `CourseOrder`, keeping the `refused` event's shape.
- **Bytes and hash** (`codec.ts`): one schema-less codec for state, orders and events, so a package adding a field to `SimState` touches nothing here. Keys are written sorted (canonical bytes whatever order fields were made in), whole 32-bit numbers as zigzag varints, every other number as its 8 IEEE bytes (-0 and NaN exact). It refuses anything not plain data, so no state can hide in a typed array or a class. `hashState` is 64 bits as 16 hex digits. A duel's state is about 3.7 KB, nearly all the sea's wave sets (25 waves a set, six numbers each, two or three sets): **for N3.7**, a state a second fits the 16 KB budget as it is; sending the sea only when `setsMade` changes would bring a state to a few hundred bytes. Encoding and hashing a state takes about 80 µs, about a step's cost.
- **Running on through orders:** `replay(state, log, untilTick, each)` with `log = [{ tick, orders }]`, what a guest does with the host's orders (N3.7) and what a saved match replays.
- **Cost:** a step of two ships is about 110 µs on the cloud machine (about 4 500 sine and cosine pairs); the test holds it under a quarter of the 2 ms budget by `pace`. Ten minutes of a duel is 18 000 steps, about 2.3 s, so the ten-minute check is a slow check (`npm run check:replay`, in CI's slow job): two runs in separate processes and one through bytes at five minutes agree at every simulated second, in the strongest wind, with the sea taking new wave sets on the way. Within one engine the state is bit-exact; across engines (the iPad's Safari and a PC's Chrome) `Math.sin`, `atan` and the like may differ in the last bit, so **N3.7** should compare hashes as a check and let the host's state win, not expect guests stepping alone to stay equal forever.

**Notes:** N3.3 (sailing model), `src/sim/ship/sailing.ts`, its numbers in `src/sim/rules/sailing.ts`.

- **Network budget.** An idle duel sends about 11 messages a second from the host (budget: 60 per player), one state of about 6 KB every 5 s. **With the crew:** two crews making sail peak at 14.7 KB a state since the crew thread keeps a man's way as what it is built from (#32), under the 16 KB. **Known limit, for the design thread:** beating to quarters (nearly every man walking, about 130 B per walking man) would pass 16 KB again. Whole states go only every 5 s, on a join or a difference, and deltas carry only orders and a hash, so it stays rare traffic and GameHub takes 1 MiB a message; if it matters, the walking men's part of the state would need to shrink again.
- **Not done here:** warming the crew's routes at load (N4.1's note): the fields are kept per JavaScript realm, so warming them in the build worker would not help the page's thread, which steps the match; N4.3 should warm them on the page behind the loading screen (or run the simulation in a worker). The fine sea's small waves are seeded from the page's first match, the same on every machine, not from the room's.
- **Checks.** `test/duel.test.ts`, `test/duelHost.test.ts` (the fake hub, `test/support/fakeHub.ts`, keeps the server's rules and sends every message through JSON). `npm run check:room`: two headless browsers on `?netcheck` play one match through `tools/net/standin.mjs` (GameHub's wire protocol on this machine; the cloud cannot reach the live server), about 15 s; with `GAMEHUB_URL` and `GAMEHUB_KEY` against the live server (CI does both once the key is the repository variable `GAMEHUB_KEY`).

**Notes:** N4.1 (crew and tasks), `src/sim/crew/` (`roster.ts`, `drill.ts`, `crew.ts`), its numbers in `src/sim/rules/crew.ts`.
- **The roster** (`ROSTER`, first proposal for G4): 121, the usual establishment of an 18-gun brig-sloop about 1805: officers and warrant and petty officers 18 (the captain, the player, is on it at the quarterdeck and never sent; the drummer counts here), 4 quartermasters (the helm), 7 idlers, 19 marines, 8 boys, and 65 seamen in parts: fore top 10, main top 12, forecastle 12, afterguard 12, waisters 19. The captain and three men of each top have their post in their top; the other 57 seamen stand at the guns, shared round the nine pairs (a pair's crew mixes the parts and stands half at each gun until she beats to quarters, N5), so making sail takes men from the guns. - **Tasks.** Each act of an order (`Act`, now known by `id`, which shares `ShipOrders.serial` with the orders) becomes its jobs (`drill`): the helm (at the wheel); for a tack or wear first "stations" (every brace and the headsail sheets manned), then the helm; a mast's braces; a square sail loosed on its yard then sheeted home (a reef: clewed up, loosed, sheeted), or clewed up; a fore-and-aft sail hoisted or doused. - **Who is given what.** The order reaches the men after `PASSED` (the master 1 s, the first lieutenant and the boatswain's pipe 2 s). Jobs are manned in the order given: for each place in turn the nearest free man of its parts (plan metres plus 3 m for each metre climbed, by the way the rig is climbed), until their skill makes up its hands. A job short of hands takes the next man freed. No man has two tasks. Men go smartly, at most `SENT_PER_STEP` (2) a ship a step; a man with no task walks back to his post.
- **The work** starts when every man told off is there (and the job before it in the act is done) and goes at the share of its hands present, never faster than with all of them; under `FEWEST` (a quarter) it does not move. So with half the hands a job takes twice its seconds. A job begun short raises the event `{ kind: 'short-handed', tick, ship, order, job }` (the first lieutenant's report, N4.5). When an act's last job is done, `carryOut(told, sailing, finishedActIds, wind)` does it to her sailing as before.
- **Times with a full crew** from their posts (force 4, beam reach, battle sail): a course 2.5 s; tack or wear to the helm over 6.4 s; back a topsail 12.8 s; heave to 11.8 s; shorten sail 10.3 s; plain sail or all sail 38 s (N3.4's table: 2, 6, 12, about 32). A man is `{ stands, going, blocked }` by his place in the roster, `stands` null at his post, so an idle crew adds about half a kilobyte. A man on his way carries only what his route is built from (route.ts `Path`: its two ends, the links taken, what was shut), with his haste, leg and share; each machine finds the route again from it on the shared walk graph (`goingRoute`, kept warm, not state). A task is its job's name and what it is done on; its places, parts, hands and work follow (`jobWork`). **A sail is set by the work itself** (design ruling N3, 2026-11-18): each sail's canvas entry carries `share` (0 furled, 1 sheeted home, rules/rig.ts `HANGING` 0.3 hanging in its gear) only while it is part way, so a steady canvas costs nothing in the state; the jobs move it (`jobSpan`: loose 0 to hanging, sheet on to 1, clew back to hanging, furl on to 0, reef stays hanging, hoist and douse the whole way) through sailing.ts `setShares`, and her drive is each sail's area by `drawShare` (a square sail draws only once sheeting starts, so clewed up it stops pushing before it is furled). The state's peak is unchanged at about 14.8 KB (a sail part way adds about 10 bytes). **Notes:** N4.2 (ways through the ship), `src/sim/crew/` (`network.ts`, `route.ts`, `going.ts`, `closures.ts`), its numbers in `src/sim/rules/routes.ts`.

**Design decisions for N5 (design thread, 2026-11-18; first proposals, the numbers live in `src/sim/rules/guns.ts` and are tuned at G5 and in N7.5):**

- **Who aims.** The duel has one enemy, so she is the target. The captain orders what to fire (round, chain, grape), at what part of her (hull, rigging, deck) and when (as they bear, or on his word); see [orders.md](orders.md). Every gun captain trains and elevates his own gun onto that part without being told. The captain never lays a gun.
- **The roll is the skill.** A gun's shot leaves along the barrel as the ship lies at that instant. "As they bear" lets each gun captain pick his own moment of the roll, with a seeded error that grows with the sea and shrinks with a full, unhurt crew. "On my word" fires every loaded gun of the side at the captain's instant: on the up-roll high into her rigging, on the down-roll low into her hull or the sea. That choice is the captain's and it matters.
- **The guns.** 32-pounder carronade: muzzle speed about 230 m/s, trains 30° either way, elevates from 5° down to 11° up, reloads in 35 s with its full crew. Long 6-pounder: about 480 m/s, trains 20° either way, the same elevation, reloads in 30 s. Short-handed, the reload stretches in proportion to the hands missing, and a gun with fewer than two men stops.
- **Range and accuracy, for pace.** Against a brig lying broadside on in a force 4 sea, with guns fired as they bear: at 100 m about three round shot in four strike her, at 200 m about half, at 400 m about one in five; beyond 600 m only the long sixes are worth firing. Chain is for her rigging out to about 250 m; grape is for her men inside about 100 m. So the fight is decided inside 200 m, closing is a choice with a cost, and the long sixes give the chase its sting.
- **Left out.** Powder and shot are not counted in the first game; no double-shotting; no misfires or burst guns.

**Open design questions for N5:** none before G5; Arnd's answer on the "Fire!" button (orders.md) decides N5.3's last part.

**Notes:** N5.1 (guns), `src/sim/guns/guns.ts`, its numbers in `src/sim/rules/guns.ts`.
- **The battery** (`battery()`): the plan's gun ports without the chase ports, larboard then starboard, each from aft: 16 carronades (A to H) and the 2 long sixes (I). A gun is known by her place in it; `SimShip.guns` holds the battery (owner guns.ts). Shifting a long six to her chase port (J) is not built: the five men the quarter bill posts at each chase port serve no gun until N5.3 shifts one there.
- **A gun's state** is packed: two whole numbers a gun in `SimShip.guns` (`Battery`), because two crews at work leave little of the 16 KB: the drill step, load, mounting and the work left in one, the training and elevation in hundredths of a degree in the other. A housed battery is 75 bytes, a working one about 185. `gunAt(guns, i)` reads a gun out as `{ drill, drillLeft, loadedWith, trained, elevated, dismounted }` (`drillLeft` in 1/1200 of a full-crew step, angles in hundredths of a degree; `drillSeconds`, `laidAt` give seconds and radians). The drill (`DrillStep`): housed → loading → ramming → loaded → running out → ready; fired: recoil → sponging → housed; a ready gun can be run in again with her load. No count of rounds is kept: a shot's scatter is seeded from the tick it is fired (N5.2).
- **What drives it:** `stepGuns(guns, men, wants)` (one `stepGun` a gun) with `want = { load, runOut, train, elevation }` (`GunWant`). Housed and told to load, she loads; loaded and told to run out, she runs out; ready and not told to, she runs in. Laying moves toward the want within her arcs at her rates (carronade 5°/s training, 2°/s elevating; long six 3°/s and 2°/s), not during the recoil. `fireGun(guns, i, men)` refuses with a reason (`cannotFire`: not loaded, not run out, dismounted, too few men) or gives the battery after the shot and the `Firing { shot, trained, elevated }` (rad) N5.2 turns into a shot. `dismountGun(guns, i)` is for N6.
- **Times:** from the shot to ready again 35 s for a carronade, 30 s for a long six with all five men (recoil 1.5 s, then the handwork shared sponge 25 %, load 30 %, ram 15 %, run out 30 %); running in 6 s. Short-handed (`STRETCH` 1): the handwork and laying take (1 + missing / 5) times as long; with fewer than two men nothing moves but the recoil.
- **Hands present** (`handsAtGuns(crew)`): men not on their way standing at one of the gun's crew places. Until she beats to quarters the quarter bill has two or three men at every gun (each pair's crew split), so every gun works at 0.7 to 0.8 pace; beating to quarters (N5.3) should send each pair's crew to the side that fights (a crew task, through crew.ts).
- **For N5.3:** `step.ts` does not step the guns yet (nothing orders them, so they stand housed): add `guns: stepGuns(s.guns, handsAtGuns(crewed.crew), wants)` with the wants from the gunnery orders, after the crew's step. `standingWants(guns)` keeps a battery as it is. A gun run in across a gangway is not put into the closures yet.


**Notes:** N5.2 (shot in flight), `src/sim/guns/shot.ts`, its numbers in `src/sim/rules/guns.ts` (`SHOT`, `MUZZLE_SPEED`, the scatter, skipping and the range table).
- **A shot** is made whole when the gun fires: `fireShot(fire)` with `{ seed, ship, gun, tick, place, firing, pose, going, men, asTheyBear, sea, t }` gives `Shot { shot, span, balls }`, each ball a `Flight { path, splashes, touches, ends }`: a point every 0.05 s from the muzzle (world metres), the instants and places it touched the water (its skips, then the plunge) and when it ended. Nothing about a shot is kept in the state while it flies. `GunFired { kind: 'fired', tick, ship, gun, fired }` is the event's shape; it is not in `SimEvent` yet (orderReport.ts's switch must learn its words when N5.3 raises it).
- **The flight:** the ball leaves the muzzle (in her port, at her side) along her bore as she lies at that instant (heading, pitch, roll), with the ship's way and her roll's own motion added; gravity and a sphere's drag by Mach number (a long six's ball leaves at Mach 1.4 and slows hard). Round shot and grape skip off the water below 6° and above 100 m/s, keeping 45 % of their speed, at most three times. Chain (two half-balls, 0.8 of the speed, four times the drag, sweeping 1.8 m) does not skip; grape is nine balls in a cone of 2.5°. Ranges to the first graze from 2.2 m: carronade 260 m at point blank (1°) and 780 m at 5°; long six 575 m and 1,335 m. The carronade's 5° range is shorter than the period's tables (its real charge drove the ball faster than the design's 230 m/s); that keeps it poor beyond 400 m as the design wants.
- **Scatter** is seeded from match, ship, gun and tick (`shotRng`): the gun's dispersion (0.6°, chain twice), the powder (2 % of speed), and, fired as they bear, the gun captain's timing on the roll (0.6 s, stretched short-handed like the drill): an error in time becomes an error in aim as fast as she rolls, so it grows with the sea. Fired on the captain's word (`asTheyBear: false`) the moment is the captain's and the timing error is left out.
- **Laying** (`layOn`): the gun captain trains on the point and elevates for its range as he judges it (seeded, 25 % one standard deviation, judged afresh every 10 s), laid against her steady heel; `elevationFor` reads a laying table built once per gun and shot (65 flights, about 40 ms per gun kind and shot the first time on the cloud machine: warm all six at load, in the worker); after that `layOn` is about 7 µs. `bears(place, train, elevation, pose, want)` says her roll brings the bore through the elevation laid for within this step: "as they bear" fires then. A gun captain on the up-roll sends it high, on the down-roll low.
- **The shares** (`npm run check:gunnery`, against her side from the water to the rail, a stand-in for N6.1): round shot as they bear in a force 4 sea strikes her 74 % at 100 m, 44 % at 200 m, 25 % at 400 m (design: 75, 50, 20). Misses close in are mostly high (low shots skip into her); far off, misjudged ranges fall short and plunge. Chain and grape carry about 370 m and 700 m at most at 100 m's laying.
- **Cost** (cloud machine): `fireShot` about 0.25 ms for round shot, 0.7 ms for grape (nine balls, skipping), 0.04 ms for chain; the sea is sampled only below the highest the water can stand (`highestWater`). A whole side of grape on the captain's word is about 6.5 ms, over a step's 2 ms: N5.3 should ripple a broadside over a few steps (two or three guns a step, as a real broadside rippled), which N5.6 hears anyway.
- **For N5.3:** each step, for each loaded gun of the side that bears, `layOn` gives the want (`train`, `laid`), `stepGuns` lays her, and when she is laid and `bears(...)` (as they bear) or at the captain's word, `fireGun` then `fireShot` with that step's pose, her sailing's `ahead` and `leeway`, the sea and `simTime`; raise `GunFired`. Aim points for hull, rigging and deck are N5.3's choice (the check aims 1.25 m above her waterline at her middle). **For N5.4:** draw each ball along its path (the points are 0.05 s apart from the firing tick) and a splash at each touch; chain whirls across its span. **For N6.1:** trace each ball's path segments through her cells as she lies at each segment's time; the path ends where the ball is spent in the sea.

A duel between two players runs to a proper end.
